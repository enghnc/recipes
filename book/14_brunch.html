Southern Smoke Brunch

Autumn · Late morning · Two kettles · Deflector

The best possible use for leftover brisket, and the argument for always cooking more than you need. Hash in cast iron with eggs cracked into the craters, biscuits under sausage gravy, grits that took an hour, and a bloody mary that sat in smoke. Everything here is heavy and everything is finished in a pan.

The menu

  • Smoked deviled eggs · Country ham with grilled peaches
  • Brisket hash with eggs · Biscuits and sausage gravy
  • Cheese grits
  • Pecan sticky buns · Smoked bloody marys

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: briquettes with one hickory chunk, used early for the eggs and the tomatoes and then left to burn out.

Kettle A — deflector at 350°F. Sticky buns first, then the grits in a Dutch oven where they can sit and be stirred occasionally for an hour. The deviled eggs and the bloody mary tomatoes take twenty minutes of light smoke at the very start, before anything else goes on.

Kettle B — cast iron, direct. Two pans running in sequence: hash with eggs, then sausage gravy. Biscuits bake on the deflector side of A alongside the buns.

Charcoal: about 2.5 chimneys per kettle.

Smoked Deviled Eggs
Appetizer · Makes 24 halves · 40 min

Twenty minutes of smoke on a peeled hard-boiled egg does something disproportionate — the white takes on colour and a faint bacon note without changing texture at all.

  • 12 eggs, boiled 10 minutes, cooled and peeled
  • ⅓ cup mayonnaise · 1 tbsp Dijon · 1 tbsp pickle relish
  • 1 tsp cider vinegar · ½ tsp smoked paprika · hot sauce
  • Chives, crisped bacon or country ham, extra paprika, to finish
  1. Set the peeled whole eggs directly on the indirect grate at 225°F with one hickory chunk. 20 minutes, no longer — past that they go rubbery and acrid.
  2. Cool, halve, and press the yolks through a sieve for a smooth filling.
  3. Mix the yolks with everything else and pipe or spoon back in, mounded high.
  4. Top with chives, a shard of crisped ham and a dusting of paprika.
To the table · platterOn a platter with a bed of coarse salt or torn lettuce to stop them sliding — smoked whites are slicker than plain ones. Filling mounded well above the white, never level. Out early, with the first drink.
Country Ham with Grilled Peaches
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 10 min
  • 8 oz country ham, sliced paper-thin
  • 6 peaches, halved and stoned
  • 2 tbsp butter · 2 tbsp honey · black pepper
  • Mint or basil
  1. Grill the peach halves cut side down over direct heat, 4 minutes, until dark and softened.
  2. Crisp the ham slices on the grate for 30 seconds a side — country ham is intensely salty and cured, so it needs heat rather than cooking.
  3. Drape the ham over the warm peaches, drizzle honey, crack pepper over, and tear herbs on top.
Brisket Hash with Eggs
Main · Serves 8 · 30 min

The rule for any hash: parboil the potatoes the day before, get the pan very hot, and then leave it alone. Stirring is what turns hash into a scramble.

  • 2 lb smoked brisket, cut into ½-inch cubes
  • 3 lb Yukon gold potatoes, parboiled, cooled and cubed
  • 2 onions and 2 bell peppers, diced
  • 4 tbsp butter or beef fat · 2 tsp smoked paprika · cayenne
  • 8 eggs · hot sauce · chives
  1. Parboil the potatoes the day before and chill them. Cold, dry cubes brown; freshly boiled ones steam and fall apart.
  2. Get a large cast-iron pan properly hot over direct heat. Butter, then the potatoes in a single layer.
  3. Do not touch them for 6 minutes. Then turn once and leave them again.
  4. Add the onions and peppers, cook 5 minutes, then fold in the brisket and the paprika and press the whole thing flat.
  5. Let a crust form underneath, 5 minutes more.
  6. Make eight craters with the back of a spoon and crack an egg into each. Move the pan to the indirect side, lid down, 5 to 7 minutes, until the whites set and the yolks stay soft.
  7. Hot sauce and chives over the top.
To the table · in the panThe skillet goes on a trivet in the middle of the table with the yolks intact and a serving spoon in it. Nobody plates a hash. Hot sauce bottles beside it, and a stack of warm biscuits so people can mop.
Biscuits and Sausage Gravy
Main · Serves 8 · 25 min
  • 1 batch buttermilk biscuits (Chapter 21)
  • 1 lb bulk breakfast sausage
  • ⅓ cup flour · 4 cups whole milk, warmed
  • 1 tbsp coarse black pepper · ½ tsp cayenne · salt
  • 2 tbsp butter, if the sausage is lean
  1. Brown the sausage in a cast-iron pan over moderate heat, breaking it up but leaving some large pieces.
  2. Sprinkle the flour over the meat and fat and cook it, stirring, for a full 2 minutes — this is the step that decides whether the gravy tastes of flour.
  3. Add the warm milk in three additions, whisking each in completely before the next.
  4. Simmer 5 to 8 minutes until it coats a spoon. It thickens more off the heat, so pull it a shade looser than you want.
  5. Far more black pepper than feels correct. Sausage gravy is a pepper dish.
  6. Split hot biscuits and ladle over.
Cheese Grits
Side · Serves 8 · 1 hr
  • 2 cups stone-ground grits (not instant, and not quick)
  • 4 cups water · 4 cups whole milk · 2 tsp salt
  • 6 tbsp butter · 8 oz sharp cheddar, grated
  • White pepper · hot sauce
  1. Bring the water, milk and salt to a simmer in a Dutch oven, then rain the grits in while whisking so they do not clump.
  2. Move to the indirect side over the deflector at 325°F and cook 45 to 60 minutes, whisking every 10 minutes and adding hot water if they tighten too far.
  3. Stone-ground grits cannot be rushed. At 20 minutes they are gritty; at an hour they are creamy, and there is no shortcut between those two states.
  4. Off the heat, beat in the butter and then the cheese in handfuls.
To the table · in the panStraight from the Dutch oven, loose enough to spread on the plate and settle — if they hold a peak they have gone too far, so slacken with hot milk before serving. A pat of butter melting on top and hot sauce beside.
Pecan Sticky Buns
Dessert · Makes 12 · 2½ hr
  • 1 batch master enriched dough (Chapter 19)
  • Goo
  • 1 stick butter · 1 cup dark brown sugar · ⅓ cup corn syrup
  • 2 tbsp heavy cream · ½ tsp salt · 2 cups pecan halves
  • Filling
  • 4 tbsp soft butter · ¾ cup brown sugar · 2 tbsp cinnamon
  1. Melt the goo ingredients together and pour into a 12-inch cast-iron skillet. Scatter the pecans over, flat side up.
  2. Roll the dough into a 16×12 rectangle, spread with the filling, and roll up tightly from the long edge.
  3. Cut into 12 with a length of dental floss slid under and pulled up through — a knife squashes the spiral.
  4. Arrange cut side up on the pecans. Proof 45 minutes until they touch.
  5. Bake over the deflector at 350°F for 30 to 35 minutes, rotating halfway, until deep gold and 190°F inside.
  6. Rest exactly 3 minutes, then invert onto a platter. Sooner and the goo runs off; later and it welds to the pan.
To the table · platterInverted in one motion so the pecans end up on top, with any goo left in the skillet scraped back over. Pulled apart by hand, not cut. Out while still warm enough that the caramel pulls a thread.
Smoked Bloody Mary
Drink · Serves 8
  • 8 tomatoes, halved and smoked 20 minutes, then blended and strained
  • 2 tbsp Worcestershire · 2 tbsp prepared horseradish
  • Juice of 2 lemons · 1 tbsp hot sauce · 2 tsp celery salt · black pepper
  • Vodka, to taste · celery, pickles, olives, lemon
  1. Smoke the tomato halves cut side up alongside the deviled eggs for 20 minutes, then blend and strain. This is the whole difference from a bottled mix.
  2. Season the base hard and chill it. It should taste slightly over-seasoned before the ice and vodka dilute it.
  3. Build over ice in tall glasses with a celery-salt rim.
  4. Also pour: bourbon-spiked coffee, and sweet tea for anyone not drinking.

Almuerzo Mexicano

Summer · Late morning · Two kettles · Comal on the grate

A Mexican late breakfast built around one thing: tortillas you pressed yourself, ten minutes before eating them. A fresh corn tortilla off a hot comal is a different food from a packaged one — it puffs, it smells of toasted corn, and it makes everything else on the table better. Chilaquiles, huevos rancheros, beans, churros, and coffee simmered with cinnamon and piloncillo in a clay pot.

The menu

  • Guacamole in the molcajete · Totopos
  • Chilaquiles · Huevos rancheros
  • Hand-made corn tortillas · Frijoles refritos · salsas (Chapter 22)
  • Churros with cajeta · Café de olla

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal. A little mesquite only if you are charring salsa vegetables; otherwise none.

Kettle A — the comal. A cast-iron griddle or a plain steel plate over a hot direct bed, held at around 450°F, running continuously. Tortillas are pressed and cooked to order in a steady stream while everything else waits.

Kettle B — pans and the fryer. Two-zone. Beans in a Dutch oven on the indirect side, chilaquiles and rancheros in cast iron over direct, then the oil pot for the churros at the end.

Charcoal: about 2 chimneys per kettle.

  • Dairy/Eggs: 16 eggs · Mexican crema · cotija · queso fresco · butter
  • Special: tortilla press · comal or flat steel plate · molcajete (optional but transformative)
  • Fuel: lump ~15 lb
Hand-Made Corn Tortillas
Side · Makes 24 · 40 min

The single highest-return thing in this chapter. Masa harina is nixtamalised corn flour — corn cooked in lime water and ground — which is why it smells of tortillas and plain cornmeal does not. Fresh masa from a tortillería is better again if you have access.

  • 2½ cups masa harina · 1 tsp salt
  • 2 cups warm water, roughly — add it by feel
  • A tortilla press and two squares of plastic cut from a freezer bag
  1. Mix the masa harina, salt and most of the water. Knead 2 minutes into a smooth dough.
  2. The feel is the recipe: it should be like soft play dough, and a ball pressed flat at the edge should crack only slightly. Too dry and the tortillas crumble; too wet and they stick to the plastic. Adjust with water a tablespoon at a time.
  3. Rest, covered, 20 minutes.
  4. Roll golf-ball-sized pieces and press between the two plastic squares to about 1/16 inch.
  5. Onto a comal at 450°F, and here is the timing that matters: 15 seconds, flip, 45 seconds, flip again, 15 seconds. On that final turn most tortillas will puff up like a balloon — that puff is the sign you got the hydration and the heat right.
  6. Stack straight into a cloth-lined basket, covered. They steam each other soft and keep for an hour.
To the table · basketIn a cloth inside a lidded basket or a tortillero, brought to the table and kept closed between servings. Made continuously through the meal rather than all at once — the person at the comal keeps working, and that is part of the event.
Guacamole in the Molcajete
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 10 min
  • 6 ripe avocados
  • 1 white onion, finely diced · 2 serranos, minced
  • 1 bunch cilantro, chopped · 2 tomatoes, seeded and diced
  • Juice of 3 limes · 1½ tsp salt
  1. Grind the onion, chilli, cilantro stems and salt to a rough paste in the molcajete first, before the avocado goes anywhere near it. This is the step that makes molcajete guacamole taste different — the aromatics are broken down rather than stirred through.
  2. Add the avocado and crush coarsely with the pestle. Leave it lumpy.
  3. Fold in the tomato, cilantro leaves and lime at the very end.
  4. Make it no more than 30 minutes ahead.
To the table · in the molcajeteMade at the table in the stone bowl it is served from, which is most of the pleasure of it. Totopos around the outside and a lime half sitting in the guacamole. Never smoothed over — it should look crushed.
Chilaquiles
Main · Serves 8 · 20 min

A dish about texture and a thirty-second window. The chips go into the sauce, get tossed, and go to the table — leave them a minute longer and you have a casserole.

  • 10 cups totopos (day-old tortillas cut in wedges and fried crisp)
  • 4 cups salsa roja or verde (Chapter 22), hot
  • 1 lb chorizo, browned (optional)
  • 4 eggs, fried, to top
  • Mexican crema · crumbled queso fresco · sliced white onion · cilantro
  1. Fry the tortilla wedges the day before until fully crisp and store them airtight. Stale chips are the only kind that work; fresh-fried ones are too fragile.
  2. Bring the salsa to a hard simmer in a wide cast-iron pan over direct heat.
  3. Add the totopos all at once and toss for 30 to 45 seconds only, until coated but still audibly crisp at the centre.
  4. Off the heat immediately. Top with fried eggs, crema in a zigzag, queso fresco, raw onion rings and cilantro.
  5. Serve within two minutes. Chilaquiles do not wait for anybody.
To the table · in the panIn the skillet, eggs on top with yolks intact, crema drizzled in a zigzag rather than dolloped, onion rings raw and white against the red. Carry it out the moment it is topped and let people serve themselves fast.
Huevos Rancheros
Main · Serves 8 · 15 min
  • 8 fresh corn tortillas
  • 8 eggs · oil or lard for frying
  • 3 cups salsa ranchera: 8 tomatoes, 2 serranos, ½ onion and 2 garlic cloves, charred and blended, then fried in lard
  • Frijoles refritos · queso fresco · cilantro · avocado
  1. Char the tomatoes, chillies, onion and garlic on the comal until blackened, blend, then fry the blended salsa in hot lard for 5 minutes. That frying step is what separates salsa ranchera from raw blended tomato.
  2. Lightly fry each tortilla on the comal with a little oil, 20 seconds a side, so it stays pliable but does not go crisp.
  3. Fry the eggs in plenty of fat, basting the tops, keeping the yolks soft.
  4. Build: tortilla, a smear of beans, the egg, then a ladle of hot salsa poured around — not over — so the yolk stays visible.
  5. Queso fresco, cilantro and sliced avocado.
Frijoles Refritos
Side · Serves 8 · 30 min plus cooking the beans
  • 1 lb dried pinto beans, cooked soft with half an onion and 2 garlic cloves, liquid reserved
  • ⅓ cup lard (or bacon fat; oil is a distant third)
  • ½ white onion, finely diced · 2 cloves garlic
  • Salt · crumbled cotija, to serve
  1. Cook the onion in the lard in a cast-iron pan until deep gold, then the garlic.
  2. Add the beans a ladleful at a time with some of their liquid, mashing as you go with a potato masher or the back of a spoon.
  3. Keep frying and mashing for 15 to 20 minutes. The beans should darken, tighten, and eventually pull away from the pan in a single mass — that is the point they are done.
  4. Loosen with bean liquid to the texture you want. Salt at the end.
Churros with Cajeta
Dessert · Makes 24 · 30 min
  • 1 cup water · 4 tbsp butter · 1 tbsp sugar · ½ tsp salt
  • 1 cup flour · 1 egg
  • Frying oil, 3 inches deep
  • 1 cup caster sugar mixed with 1 tbsp cinnamon
  • Cajeta, warmed, for dipping
  1. Bring the water, butter, sugar and salt to a boil, tip in the flour all at once, and beat hard over the heat for 2 minutes until it forms a smooth ball.
  2. Cool 5 minutes, then beat in the egg. The paste should be stiff enough to hold a ridge.
  3. Pipe 5-inch lengths through a large star nozzle — the ridges are structural, not decorative, and a plain nozzle gives a churro that can explode in the oil.
  4. Fry at 350°F for 2 to 3 minutes a side, until deep gold.
  5. Drain briefly and roll in cinnamon sugar while still hot, so it sticks.
  6. Serve immediately with warm cajeta.
To the table · basketPiled in a paper-lined basket, sugar still visibly clinging, with cajeta in small cups — one per person, because everyone dips and nobody wants to share. Fried to order, in rounds, while the coffee goes round.
Café de Olla & Agua de Melón
Drinks · Serves 8

Coffee simmered with cinnamon and raw cane sugar in a clay pot. The clay matters — it rounds the coffee in a way metal does not — but a Dutch oven on the embers gets you most of the way.

  • 8 cups water · 6 oz piloncillo, chopped
  • 2 cinnamon sticks · 1 star anise · a strip of orange peel
  • ¾ cup coarsely ground coffee
  • Agua de melón
  • ½ melon, chopped · 6 cups water · ⅓ cup sugar · juice of 1 lime
  1. Simmer the water, piloncillo, cinnamon, anise and peel in a clay olla or Dutch oven set in the embers for 10 minutes, until the sugar dissolves.
  2. Take it off the fire, stir in the coffee, cover, and steep 5 minutes. Do not boil the coffee.
  3. Strain and serve in clay cups or mugs. Keep the pot near the fire and refill all morning.
  4. For the agua, blend the melon with the water and sugar, strain, add lime, and serve very cold over ice.
  5. Also pour: a michelada for anyone who wants one — lager, lime, hot sauce, Worcestershire and a salted rim.
