Tuscan Harvest

Autumn · Dinner party · Two kettles · Rotisserie + pizza stone

Late September in Italy. A rolled porchetta turns slowly over coals for three hours while the second kettle runs as a wood-fired oven — flatbread to start, a lattice crostata to finish. The seasoning is restrained to the point of austerity: garlic, fennel, rosemary, sage, lemon, pepper, salt. Nothing sweet, nothing smoky.

The menu

  • Fettunta · Grilled radicchio and fennel · a board of pecorino and finocchiona
  • Rotisserie porchetta
  • Fagioli all'uccelletto · Schiacciata (Chapter 20) · Grilled cavolo nero
  • Crostata di marmellata (pastry, Chapter 21) with cantucci and Vin Santo

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with 3 oak chunks, or olive wood if you can get it.

Kettle A — the spit. Split baskets with a drip pan between, running 325°F. Porchetta on for 2½ to 3 hours to 145–150°F in the loin, fed 8–10 briquettes per side every 45 minutes. For the last 15 minutes, pull the drip pan, rake both baskets into one pile directly under the roast, take the lid off, and let the skin crackle.

Kettle B — stone, then deflector. Full chimney with the stone in from cold, 30 minutes to preheat. Schiacciata first at around 600°F. Then let the fire fall back, slide in the deflector, and hold 375°F for the beans and the crostata.

Charcoal: about 2.5 chimneys on A including top-ups, 1.5 on B.

Fettunta
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 8 min

Literally "oiled slice" — the Tuscan ancestor of bruschetta, made to show off new-pressing olive oil. Use far more oil than feels reasonable. That is the dish.

  • 1 loaf pane toscano or country sourdough, cut into thick slices
  • 3 cloves garlic, halved
  • Very good olive oil, generously
  • Flaky sea salt
  1. Grill the bread over live embers until well charred on both sides and still soft inside, about 90 seconds a side.
  2. While it is still hot, rub the cut face of a garlic clove firmly across the rough charred surface — the char acts as the grater.
  3. Pour olive oil over until it soaks in and pools slightly. Salt heavily. Serve at once.
Grilled Radicchio and Fennel
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 15 min
  • 3 heads radicchio, quartered through the core
  • 3 fennel bulbs, cut into ½-inch wedges through the root
  • ⅓ cup olive oil · salt and pepper
  • 3 tbsp aged balsamic · shaved pecorino
  1. Leave the core intact on both — it holds the wedges together on the grate.
  2. Brush with oil, season, and grill over medium direct heat, 4 to 5 minutes per side, until charred at the edges and just softened.
  3. Radicchio is bitter and gets more so with heat; pull it while it still has some crispness at the heart.
  4. Drizzle with balsamic and shave pecorino over while warm.
Rotisserie Porchetta
Main · Serves 8 with leftovers · 3 hr plus overnight

A loin wrapped in belly gives you both lean meat and the fat and skin that make porchetta what it is. The single most important step happens the night before: the skin must dry out completely, or it will never crackle.

  • 4 lb boneless pork loin
  • 3 lb skin-on pork belly, roughly the same length
  • 8 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp fennel pollen, or 3 tbsp fennel seed, toasted and crushed
  • 3 tbsp chopped rosemary · 3 tbsp chopped sage
  • Zest of 2 lemons
  • 2 tbsp coarse black pepper
  • 3 tbsp coarse salt, plus more for the skin
  • Butcher's twine
  1. Lay the belly skin-side down. Score the flesh in a shallow crosshatch. Score the skin in narrow parallel lines, cutting through the skin but not into the fat.
  2. Mix the garlic, fennel, herbs, zest, pepper and salt. Rub it over the belly flesh and all over the loin.
  3. Lay the loin along one long edge of the belly and roll it up tightly. Tie firmly at 1-inch intervals, and don't be gentle — a loose roll cooks unevenly and won't hold on the spit.
  4. Rub the skin with coarse salt and refrigerate uncovered, overnight. The skin should feel dry and papery in the morning.
  5. Spit it through the centre, tighten the forks hard, and check the balance across two glasses before lighting anything.
  6. Roast indirect at 325°F for 2½ to 3 hours, to 145–150°F in the loin.
  7. The crackling blast: remove the drip pan, consolidate all the coals directly under the roast, lid off, and let it spin for 10 to 15 minutes. The skin will blister, bubble and pop audibly. Watch it — this is the only part that can go wrong fast.
  8. Rest 20 minutes, cut the twine, and slice into thick rounds so each slice has skin, fat and loin.
Fagioli all'Uccelletto
Side · Serves 8 · 45 min

"Beans cooked like a little bird" — meaning with the sage and garlic you'd use for game. Whole sage leaves, left in, are essential.

  • 3 cans cannellini beans, drained and rinsed (or 1 lb dried, soaked and simmered)
  • ⅓ cup olive oil
  • 5 cloves garlic, lightly crushed
  • 12 whole sage leaves
  • 1 can San Marzano tomatoes, crushed by hand
  • Salt, black pepper, a pinch of chilli flakes
  1. Warm the oil with the garlic and sage in a Dutch oven over moderate direct heat until fragrant but not browned, 2 minutes.
  2. Add the tomatoes and cook down 10 minutes.
  3. Add the beans, season, and move to the indirect side at 350°F, lid ajar, 30 to 40 minutes, until thickened and glossy.
  4. Finish with a hard pour of raw olive oil. Serve warm, not hot.
Grilled Cavolo Nero
Side · Serves 8 · 6 min
  • 2 bunches cavolo nero (lacinato kale), thick stems stripped
  • 3 tbsp olive oil · 2 cloves garlic, grated
  • Lemon · salt · chilli flakes
  1. Toss the whole leaves in oil and salt.
  2. Lay them flat on the grate over medium heat, 90 seconds a side, until the edges crisp and blacken and the centres wilt.
  3. Pile up, toss with grated garlic, lemon juice and chilli while hot.
Crostata di Marmellata
Dessert · Serves 8 · 40 min
  • 1 batch pasta frolla (Chapter 21)
  • 1½ cups thick apricot or sour-cherry jam
  • 1 egg, beaten, for the lattice
  • Cantucci and Vin Santo, to serve
  1. Press two-thirds of the chilled pastry into a 10-inch foil tart pan or cast-iron skillet, up the sides.
  2. Spread the jam evenly. If it's loose, stir in a teaspoon of cornstarch first.
  3. Roll the remaining third, cut into ¾-inch strips, and lay them in a lattice. Brush with egg.
  4. Bake over the deflector at 375°F for 35 to 40 minutes, rotating halfway, until sandy gold and the jam is bubbling at the edges.
  5. Cool at least 30 minutes — it sets as it cools. Serve with cantucci for dunking in Vin Santo.
Sage-Lemon Shrub & What to Pour
Drinks · Serves 8
  • 1 cup sugar · 1 cup water · 20 sage leaves · 3 strips lemon peel
  • ¾ cup white wine vinegar · juice of 3 lemons
  • Soda water, ice
  1. Simmer the sugar, water, sage and peel in a small pot on the grill for 5 minutes. Cool, then strain.
  2. Stir in the vinegar and lemon juice. Keeps a month refrigerated.
  3. Pour 1½ oz into a glass of ice and top with soda.
  4. Also pour: Chianti Classico with the porchetta — its acid and tannin cut the fat; a Rosso di Montalcino if you want more weight. Vin Santo with the cantucci. A Negroni as aperitivo. Chinotto over ice with an orange twist for anyone not drinking.

Pollo a la Brasa

Spring · Birthday · One kettle · Rotisserie

Peru's national weeknight dinner, and the simplest menu in this book to execute: two chickens on a spit, potatoes roasting in the fat below them, and two sauces made in a blender the day before. Everything can be done on a single kettle, which makes this the menu to cook the first time you use a rotisserie.

The menu

  • Anticuchos · Choclo con queso
  • Pollo a la brasa (marinade, Chapter 24)
  • Papas a la brasa · Ensalada criolla · Ají verde and ají amarillo cream (Chapter 22)
  • Picarones with chancaca syrup

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal. No smoke wood — this is a roast, not barbecue.

One kettle, start to finish. Light a full chimney, split it into two side baskets, and put the drip pan with the potato wedges in the channel between them. Spit both chickens, trussed tight and facing opposite directions for balance. Hold 325–350°F for 75 to 90 minutes, to 165°F in the thigh. Pull the chickens to rest, take off the spit, rake the coals into one bed, and grill the anticuchos and corn hot and fast — 2 to 3 minutes a side — then fry the picarones in a Dutch oven over the same fire. Courses arrive slightly out of order and nobody minds.

Two kettles if you'd rather serve appetizers first: rotisserie on A, a half-chimney direct fire on B for the skewers and corn.

Charcoal: about 2 chimneys total for the one-kettle version.

  • Pantry: ají panca paste · ají amarillo paste · huacatay paste · soy sauce · red wine vinegar · cumin · dried oregano · 1 bottle dark beer · mayonnaise · flour · yeast · anise seed · chancaca or piloncillo (or dark brown sugar + molasses) · frying oil · dried purple corn, cinnamon sticks and cloves for chicha morada · sugar · skewers
  • Dairy: queso fresco · cotija · evaporated milk
  • Fuel: lump ~15 lb
Anticuchos
Appetizer · Makes 16 skewers · 8 min

Beef heart is the traditional cut and it is excellent — lean, mineral, and closer to a good steak than to offal. If it's a bridge too far, sirloin works and nobody at the table will raise it.

  • 2 lb beef heart, trimmed of all silverskin and fat, cut into 1-inch cubes (or sirloin)
  • Anticucho marinade (Chapter 24)
  • Reserved marinade thinned with oil, for basting
  • Boiled potato and corn, to serve alongside
  1. Trim the heart ruthlessly — every bit of membrane left on will be chewy.
  2. Marinate overnight. Thread 4 or 5 cubes per skewer, packed close.
  3. Grill over a very hot direct fire, 2 to 3 minutes a side, basting with the reserved marinade at each turn.
  4. Serve medium — beef heart past medium turns tough.
Choclo con Queso
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 12 min
  • 8 ears corn, husked
  • 8 oz queso fresco, cut into slabs
  • 2 tbsp oil · lime · salt
  1. Grill the corn over direct heat, turning, 10 minutes, until lightly charred.
  2. Grill the cheese slabs 1 minute a side in a hot cast-iron pan until they mark but hold shape.
  3. Serve corn and cheese together with lime and salt. Peruvian choclo has enormous starchy kernels; sweetcorn is a fine substitute but char it harder.
Pollo a la Brasa
Main · Serves 8 · 90 min plus overnight
  • 2 whole chickens, about 4 lb each
  • Pollo a la brasa marinade (Chapter 24)
  • Ají verde and ají amarillo cream (Chapter 22)
  1. Marinate 12 to 24 hours, working the marinade under the breast skin and into the cavity.
  2. Pat the skin dry. A wet bird steams and never lacquers, no matter how good the fire is.
  3. Truss tightly — wings pinned, legs tied — and spit both birds facing opposite directions so the weight balances.
  4. Indirect at 325–350°F, drip pan below. 75 to 90 minutes, feeding the baskets once at the 45-minute mark.
  5. Stop the motor and probe the thigh: 165°F. The skin should be dark mahogany and tight.
  6. Rest 15 minutes, then cut into quarters and serve with both sauces and a heap of potatoes.
Papas a la Brasa & Ensalada Criolla
Sides · Serves 8
  • Potatoes
  • 4 lb Yukon gold potatoes, cut into thick wedges
  • 2 tbsp oil · 1 tsp cumin · salt and pepper
  • Ensalada criolla
  • 3 red onions, sliced paper-thin
  • 1 ají amarillo or 2 jalapeños, sliced
  • Juice of 4 limes · ½ bunch cilantro · salt
  1. Toss the wedges in oil and cumin and spread them in the drip pan beneath the chickens for the final 50 minutes. They roast in the rendering fat, which is the point.
  2. If they need more colour, spread them on the grate over the raked coals for 5 minutes at the end.
  3. For the salad, soak the sliced onion in cold water 10 minutes and drain — this takes out the harshness and leaves the crunch. Toss with chilli, lime, cilantro and salt no more than 30 minutes before serving.
Picarones with Chancaca Syrup
Dessert · Serves 8 · 40 min

Peruvian squash-and-sweet-potato doughnuts, fried in a Dutch oven right on the grill. The batter is loose and sticky — that's correct, and it's what gives them their light, slightly chewy interior.

  • 1 sweet potato and ½ small pumpkin or butternut, about 1½ lb total
  • 1 tsp anise seed
  • 2¼ tsp instant yeast · 1 tbsp sugar
  • 3 cups all-purpose flour · 1 tsp salt
  • Frying oil, 3 inches deep
  • Chancaca syrup
  • 8 oz chancaca or piloncillo, chopped (or 1 cup dark brown sugar + 1 tbsp molasses)
  • 1 cup water · 1 cinnamon stick · 3 cloves · peel of 1 orange · 2 fig leaves or a strip of lime peel
  1. Boil the squash and sweet potato with the anise until very soft. Drain, reserving a cup of the liquid, and mash smooth.
  2. Stir the yeast and sugar into the warm reserved liquid, then beat into the purée with the flour and salt. You want a thick, sticky, slack batter.
  3. Cover and rise 1½ to 2 hours until visibly bubbly.
  4. Simmer the syrup ingredients in a small pot on the grill for 20 minutes until it coats a spoon. Strain.
  5. Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over direct heat to 350°F and hold it there. With wet hands, scoop batter and form a ring by poking a thumb through the middle, then drop it in.
  6. Fry 2 to 3 minutes per side until deep gold. Drain and pour warm syrup over.
Frying on a grill

Never leave a pot of hot oil unattended on live coals, never fill it more than a third full, and keep the lid nearby to smother a fire. Move the pot to the indirect side to cool it if the temperature climbs past 375°F.

Pisco Sour & Chicha Morada
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Pisco sour · per glass
  • 2 oz pisco · ¾ oz lime juice · ¾ oz simple syrup · 1 egg white · 3 drops Angostura
  • Chicha morada · serves 8
  • 1 lb dried purple corn · 10 cups water
  • Peel of 1 pineapple · 2 cinnamon sticks · 6 cloves
  • ¾ cup sugar · juice of 4 limes · diced apple, to finish
  1. Chicha: simmer the corn, pineapple peel, cinnamon and cloves in the water for 45 minutes in a pot on the grill. Strain, sweeten, cool completely, then add lime and diced apple. Serve very cold — this is the drink of the meal.
  2. Pisco sour: shake everything except the bitters without ice first for 15 seconds to build the foam, then add ice and shake again. Strain and dot the foam with bitters.
  3. Also pour: Inca Kola, which is the culturally correct pairing whatever anyone says about it; Cusqueña lager; a chilcano (pisco, ginger ale, lime) for the long afternoon.

Punjabi Tandoori

Winter · Celebration · Two kettles · Pizza stone + deflector

The pizza stone is the whole trick. Preheated hard under a closed lid it reaches genuine tandoor floor temperature, and naan slapped straight onto it balloons and blisters exactly as it should. Rich, warming, and the heaviest menu in the book to prep — but almost all of it happens the day before.

The menu

  • Chicken malai tikka · Paneer tikka · Papad
  • Tandoori chicken (double marinade, Chapter 24)
  • Naan and garlic naan (Chapter 20) · Dal makhani · Kachumber · Jeera rice
  • Gajar ka halwa

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal, and a lot of it — the stone is hungry. No smoke wood. A tandoor is a clean-burning clay oven; there is no wood smoke in real tandoori food. For smokiness, use the dhungar method on the finished dal instead (Chapter 6).

Kettle A — the naan station. Stone in from cold, a full chimney of lump spread beneath it, lid down, 30 to 40 minutes to reach a stone surface of 600–700°F. Naan takes 60 to 90 seconds each. Let the stone recover for two minutes every fourth bread.

Kettle B — tikka, then the deflector. Start with a hot direct bed for the skewers and the tandoori chicken, then slide the deflector in, drop to 325°F, and finish the chicken through while the dal and rice hold on the same indirect grate.

Charcoal: about 2 chimneys per kettle, possibly more on A.

Chicken Malai Tikka
Appetizer · Makes 16 skewers · 15 min

The pale, creamy counterpart to red tandoori chicken — no chilli powder, no colour, just cream, cashew and green chilli. Serve it alongside the red for contrast.

  • 2 lb boneless chicken thigh, in 1½-inch pieces
  • ½ cup hung yogurt · ½ cup heavy cream
  • ¼ cup cashews, soaked and blended smooth
  • 3 green chillies and 2 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 tsp white pepper, 1 tsp garam masala, ½ tsp ground cardamom
  • 2 tbsp gram flour, lightly toasted · 2 tsp salt
  • Ghee, for basting
  1. Blend the marinade smooth and coat the chicken. Refrigerate 4 to 12 hours.
  2. Thread onto skewers, leaving small gaps.
  3. Grill over hot direct heat, turning every 2 minutes, 10 to 12 minutes, basting with ghee.
  4. You want pale gold with dark blistered spots — not evenly browned. Pull at 175°F.
Paneer Tikka & Papad
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 12 min
  • 1½ lb paneer, in 1½-inch cubes
  • 1 cup hung yogurt · 2 tbsp tandoori masala (Chapter 23)
  • 2 tbsp mustard oil · 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste · juice of 1 lemon · salt
  • 2 bell peppers and 1 onion, in squares
  • 16 papad · chaat masala
  1. Marinate the paneer 1 hour only — longer and the acid makes it crumbly.
  2. Skewer alternating with pepper and onion. Grill over direct heat, 2 minutes a side, 8 minutes total. Paneer doesn't need cooking, only colouring.
  3. Lay papad flat on the bare grate for 15 to 20 seconds a side. They crisp and curl almost instantly and burn a second later. Dust with chaat masala.
Tandoori Chicken
Main · Serves 8 · 40 min plus marinating
  • 1 whole chicken cut into 8, plus 3 lb bone-in thighs and drumsticks
  • Tandoori double marinade (Chapter 24)
  • Ghee, for basting · sliced onion, lemon and chaat masala, to serve
  1. Skin the chicken — tandoori chicken is always skinless, so the marinade reaches the meat.
  2. Slash each piece to the bone three times. Apply the first marinade, 30 to 45 minutes, then the second, 6 to 24 hours.
  3. Let the excess drip off before grilling. A thick sheet of yogurt steams rather than chars.
  4. Over hot direct heat: 6 to 8 minutes a side, until well blackened in patches.
  5. Slide the deflector into Kettle B, drop to 325°F, and finish, lid down, 15 to 20 minutes to 175°F in the thigh, basting with ghee twice.
  6. Serve on a bed of raw sliced onion with lemon wedges and a dusting of chaat masala.
Dal Makhani
Side · Serves 8 · Overnight soak plus 4 hr

Time is the ingredient. The dal should sit at a bare murmur for hours until the lentils break down into something closer to a sauce than a soup, and it will taste noticeably better if made a day ahead.

  • 1½ cups whole black urad dal · ¼ cup kidney beans, soaked overnight
  • 6 tbsp butter · 2 tbsp ghee
  • 1 onion, finely chopped · 2 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 4 tomatoes, puréed, or 1 can
  • 1 tbsp Kashmiri chilli powder · 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp cumin
  • 1 tbsp kasoori methi, crushed
  • ½ cup heavy cream · salt
  • 1 lump charcoal and 1 tsp ghee, for the dhungar
  1. Drain the pulses, cover with 6 cups fresh water, and simmer in a Dutch oven until completely soft — 1½ to 2 hours. Mash a third of them against the side.
  2. In ghee, cook the onion until deep gold, then the ginger-garlic, then the tomato and spices until the fat separates out.
  3. Combine with the dal, add the butter, and move to the indirect side at 300°F for 2 to 3 hours, lid ajar, stirring occasionally and adding water as needed.
  4. Stir in the cream and kasoori methi in the last 15 minutes. Salt to taste.
  5. Dhungar: set a small foil bowl in the middle of the dal, drop in one glowing ember, pour a teaspoon of ghee over it, and clamp the lid on for 2 minutes. Remove the bowl and stir.
Jeera Rice & Kachumber
Sides · Serves 8
  • Rice
  • 3 cups basmati, rinsed and soaked 30 min
  • 2 tbsp ghee · 1 tbsp cumin seed · 2 bay leaves · 4 cloves
  • 4½ cups water · salt
  • Kachumber
  • 3 cucumbers, 4 tomatoes, 1 red onion, all finely diced
  • 1 green chilli · juice of 2 limes · 1 tsp chaat masala · cilantro · salt
  1. Bloom the cumin, bay and cloves in ghee in a Dutch oven over direct heat until the seeds sizzle and darken.
  2. Add the drained rice and toss for a minute, then the water and salt. Bring to a boil, cover, move to the indirect side and cook 12 minutes undisturbed. Rest 10 minutes off the heat, then fork through.
  3. Toss the kachumber ingredients no more than 20 minutes before serving — it weeps quickly and should be crisp.
Gajar ka Halwa
Dessert · Serves 8 · 1 hr on the grill
  • 3 lb carrots, grated
  • 6 cups whole milk
  • ¾ cup sugar · ⅓ cup ghee
  • 1 tsp ground green cardamom
  • ½ cup chopped almonds, cashews and pistachios
  • 2 tbsp raisins (optional)
  1. Combine the carrots and milk in a wide cast-iron pan over moderate direct heat. Simmer, stirring every few minutes, until the milk has almost entirely evaporated — 35 to 45 minutes. This reduction is the dish; there is no shortcut.
  2. Add the ghee and sugar and cook another 10 to 15 minutes, stirring more often now, until the mixture pulls away from the sides and glistens.
  3. Stir in the cardamom, nuts and raisins.
  4. Serve warm, in small bowls. It reheats perfectly.
Lassi, Nimbu Pani & Masala Chai
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Salted lassi
  • 3 cups yogurt · 2 cups cold water · 1 tsp salt · ½ tsp roasted cumin · mint
  • Mango lassi
  • 3 cups yogurt · 2 cups mango pulp · ¼ cup sugar · ½ tsp cardamom
  • Nimbu pani
  • Juice of 8 limes · ½ cup sugar · 1 tsp salt · ½ tsp roasted cumin · 6 cups water or soda
  • Masala chai
  • 4 cups water, 3 cups milk · 4 tbsp black tea · 6 cardamom pods, 1 cinnamon stick, 4 cloves, 1-inch ginger, crushed · sugar
  1. Blend each lassi until frothy and serve over ice. Make both — the split between salted and sweet is always about even.
  2. For the chai, boil the water with the crushed spices and ginger for 5 minutes, add the tea for 2 minutes, then the milk and sugar and bring back to a boil twice. Strain. Do this in a pot on the grate after dinner.
  3. Also pour: off-dry Riesling — the sweetness tames the chilli and the acid handles the cream; Kingfisher lager; a cardamom-and-lime gin and tonic.

Jamaican Jerk

Summer · All-day cookout · Two kettles · Deflector

Real jerk is smoke-roasting rather than grilling: pork cooked slowly over green pimento wood until the skin is lacquered almost black and the meat chops apart. The deflector does the heavy lifting for seven hours while you do almost nothing.

The menu

  • Jerk wings (dry rub, Chapter 23) · Festival · Grilled plantain
  • Jerk pork shoulder (jerk paste, Chapter 22) · Jerk chicken
  • Rice and peas · Mango salsa · Steamed cabbage and carrot
  • Sweet potato pudding and rum bananas

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with pimento (allspice) wood — the defining flavour. If you can't source it, scatter a tablespoon of whole allspice berries on the coals and use oak with a little cherry.

Kettle A — the pork. Deflector in, coals ringed, drip pan of water beneath. 275–300°F for 6 to 7 hours to 200–205°F. Wood in the first two hours only. Top up 10 to 12 briquettes per hour. No wrap — you want a dark, dry, spicy bark.

Kettle B — chicken, wings, sides. Deflector at 325°F for the chicken quarters, then pull the deflector for a direct char at the end. Rice and peas in a Dutch oven on the indirect side. Plantains, wings and the banana packets over the fading coals.

One-kettle version: run the pork all day, hold it wrapped in a cooler, then rebuild a hot fire for chicken and sides. Drop the wings.

Charcoal: around 4 chimneys on A across the day, 2 on B.

Prep countdown

Jerk Pork Shoulder
Main · Serves 8 with leftovers · 7 hr plus overnight
  • 1 bone-in pork shoulder, about 6 lb
  • 2 cups jerk paste (Chapter 22)
  • Pimento wood chunks
  • Reserved jerk paste thinned with lime juice and oil, for the table
  1. Score the fat cap in a deep crosshatch and stab the shoulder all over with a knife tip.
  2. Wear gloves and work the paste into every cut and crevice. Bag and refrigerate 12 to 24 hours.
  3. Deflector in, water pan beneath, kettle stabilised at 275–300°F. Pork on, fat side up, pimento wood on the coals.
  4. Cook 6 to 7 hours to 200–205°F, adding 10 to 12 briquettes an hour. Do not wrap and do not spritz — jerk bark should be dry and crusted, not sticky.
  5. Rest 30 minutes, then chop with a heavy knife rather than pulling. Jerk pork is chopped, bark and all, so every forkful has crust in it.
  6. Toss with a few spoonfuls of the reserved thinned paste and serve with more alongside.
Jerk Chicken & Jerk Wings
Main and appetizer · Serves 8 · 1½ hr
  • 4 lb chicken leg quarters · 1 cup jerk paste
  • 3 lb wings · 3 tbsp dry jerk rub (Chapter 23) · 1 tbsp baking powder
  • Lime wedges
  1. Score the quarters and coat with jerk paste. Marinate 4 to 12 hours.
  2. Deflector in at 325°F: chicken skin-side up, 1 hour 10 to 1 hour 20 to 175°F in the thigh.
  3. Pull the deflector, rake the coals, and char the quarters skin-side down for 3 to 4 minutes to blister.

Rice and Peas
Side · Serves 8 · 45 min

"Peas" means kidney beans, and the whole Scotch bonnet goes in intact — it perfumes the pot without releasing its heat, provided nobody bursts it.

  • 1 can kidney beans, with their liquid (or 1 cup dried, cooked)
  • 3 cups long-grain rice, rinsed
  • 2 cans coconut milk · 2 cups water
  • 4 scallions, bruised whole · 6 sprigs thyme
  • 3 cloves garlic · 1 tsp whole allspice
  • 1 whole Scotch bonnet, unpierced · 2 tsp salt
  1. Bring the coconut milk, water, beans, aromatics and whole Scotch bonnet to a boil in a Dutch oven over direct heat. Simmer 10 minutes.
  2. Stir in the rinsed rice and salt. Bring back to a boil.
  3. Cover, move to the indirect side, and cook 25 minutes undisturbed.
  4. Rest 10 minutes off the heat. Remove the pepper whole and intact, then fork through.
Festival
Side · Makes 16 · 25 min
  • 1½ cups cornmeal · 1½ cups flour
  • ⅓ cup sugar · 1 tbsp baking powder · 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp vanilla · ¾ to 1 cup water
  • Frying oil, 3 inches deep

Part Five · Spit, Stone & Deflector

Alsatian Christmas

Winter · Holiday · Two kettles · Rotisserie + pizza stone

Cold-weather grilling with a holiday table in mind. A capon turns on the spit for two and a half hours while tartes flambées come off the stone in two minutes each — which means guests eat continuously, standing up, while the centrepiece takes its time. Elegant, and nothing else in this book resembles it.

The menu

  • Tarte flambée (base, Chapter 20) · Bacon-wrapped prunes · charcuterie
  • Rotisserie capon with Riesling pan sauce
  • Choucroute · Root vegetables from the drip pan · Buttered spaetzle
  • Sweet tarte flambée and baked apples

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: briquettes, which behave far better than lump in the cold, plus 2 apple or cherry chunks. Below 40°F, budget 25 to 30 percent more fuel, shelter the kettle from wind, and resist lifting the lid.

Kettle A — the spit. Split baskets at 350°F with a drip pan between them — put the root vegetables in it and they roast in the fat. An 8 lb capon takes 2 to 2½ hours to 165°F, basted with thyme butter every 30 minutes, fed 10 briquettes per side every 45.

Kettle B — stone, then deflector. Full chimney with the stone preheating from cold; 550–600°F for the tartes, which take 2 to 4 minutes each and are made to order. Then swap to the deflector at 325°F for the choucroute, the spaetzle pan and the baked apples.

Charcoal: about 3 chimneys on A with the cold allowance, 2 on B.

Tarte Flambée flammekueche
Appetizer · Makes 6 · 4 min each

Topped sparingly and rolled almost transparent. The most common mistake is treating it like a pizza — too much cream, too much bacon, and the base steams instead of crisping.

  • 1 batch tarte flambée base (Chapter 20)
  • 1 cup fromage blanc · ½ cup crème fraîche
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil · nutmeg, salt, white pepper
  • 2 onions, sliced as thinly as you can manage
  • 10 oz lardons or thick bacon, cut into batons
  1. Whisk the fromage blanc, crème fraîche and oil with a grating of nutmeg, salt and pepper. It should be spreadable, not runny.
  2. Roll a piece of dough paper-thin into a rough oval — irregular shapes are traditional.
  3. Spread a thin layer of the cream mixture, leaving a bare half-inch border. Scatter onion and lardons sparsely.
  4. Slide onto the stone at 550–600°F, lid down. 2 to 4 minutes, until the edges blacken in spots and the base is crisp.
  5. Cut into strips with scissors and hand them straight out. Then make the next one.
Bacon-Wrapped Prunes
Appetizer · Makes 16 · 12 min
  • 16 large prunes · 16 blanched almonds
  • 8 rashers thin bacon, halved
  • Black pepper · 2 tbsp Armagnac or Calvados (optional)
  1. If using spirits, soak the prunes in them for an hour first.
  2. Push an almond into each prune, wrap in a half rasher, and secure with a toothpick.
  3. Grill over indirect heat, turning, 10 to 12 minutes, until the bacon is crisp. Direct heat renders the fat too fast and it flares.
Rotisserie Capon with Riesling Pan Sauce
Main · Serves 8 · 2½ hr plus overnight
  • 1 capon, about 8 lb (or a large roasting chicken, or 2 ducks)
  • Kosher salt, 1 tsp per pound
  • 1 apple and 1 onion, quartered, for the cavity
  • Thyme butter
  • 1 stick butter, melted · 2 tbsp thyme leaves · 1 tsp crushed juniper · 2 cloves garlic
  • Pan sauce
  • Drip pan juices · 1 cup dry Riesling · 2 tbsp butter · 1 tsp Dijon · lemon
  1. Salt the bird all over and under the breast skin and leave it uncovered in the fridge overnight. This is the difference between good skin and great skin.
  2. Stuff the cavity with apple and onion, truss tightly, spit, and balance.
  3. Indirect at 350°F, drip pan with the root vegetables beneath. 2 to 2½ hours to 165°F in the thigh, basting with thyme butter every 30 minutes.
  4. Rest 20 minutes on a board.
  5. Pour the drip pan juices into a skillet, skim most of the fat, add the Riesling and reduce hard by half over direct heat. Whisk in the butter and mustard off the heat, sharpen with lemon.
Choucroute
Side · Serves 8 · 2 hr
  • 2 lb sauerkraut, rinsed once and squeezed
  • 4 oz lardons · 2 onions, sliced
  • 1 lb smoked sausage, in chunks
  • 1½ cups dry Riesling · 1 cup stock
  • 1 tbsp juniper berries · 2 bay leaves · 1 tsp peppercorns · 4 cloves
  • 1 apple, grated · 2 tbsp butter
  1. Render the lardons in a Dutch oven over direct heat, then soften the onions in the fat.
  2. Add the sauerkraut, wine, stock, grated apple and the spices tied in a scrap of cloth.
  3. Move to the indirect side at 325°F, lid on, and cook 1½ to 2 hours, checking the liquid twice.
  4. Bury the sausage in the kraut for the last 30 minutes. Stir in the butter before serving.
Drip-Pan Root Vegetables & Crisped Spaetzle
Sides · Serves 8

  • 2 tbsp honey · thyme · salt and pepper
  • 1 lb spaetzle, cooked · 4 tbsp butter · parsley
  1. Put the vegetables in the drip pan under the capon for the final 75 minutes. They roast in the rendering fat and need nothing else.
  2. Toss with honey and thyme when they come out, and finish over direct heat for 3 minutes if you want more colour.
  3. For the spaetzle, melt butter in a cast-iron pan over direct heat and fry the cooked spaetzle hard, undisturbed, until the underside is crisp — about 5 minutes — then toss and repeat. Parsley at the end.
Sweet Tarte Flambée & Baked Apples
Dessert · Serves 8 · 30 min
  • 2 pieces of tarte flambée dough
  • ¾ cup crème fraîche · 2 tbsp sugar
  • 3 apples, sliced paper-thin · cinnamon sugar
  • 3 tbsp Calvados, to flame
  • Baked apples
  • 8 baking apples, cored · ½ cup brown sugar
  • 4 tbsp butter · ½ cup raisins · 1 tsp cinnamon
  1. Stuff the cored apples with butter, brown sugar, raisins and cinnamon. Stand them in a foil pan with a splash of water and bake over the deflector at 350°F for 35 to 45 minutes, until collapsing.

Part Five · Spit, Stone & Deflector

Hanoi Bún Chả

Late summer · Casual · One kettle · No accessories

The lightest menu in the book and the easiest to run. No deflector, no spit, no stone — just a hot bed of coals, thin pork, and a table covered in noodles, herbs and dipping bowls. Everything is assembled by the eater, which means you cook and they work.

The menu

  • Nem nướng · Bắp nướng mỡ hành (grilled corn with scallion oil)
  • Bún chả (marinade, Chapter 24; nước chấm, Chapter 22)
  • Herb platter · Rice vermicelli · Đồ chua · Grilled eggplant with scallion oil
  • Chuối nướng — sticky-rice grilled bananas with coconut cream

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal, full and hot. No smoke wood.

One kettle, direct, high. Light a full chimney and spread it across two-thirds of the grate, leaving a third bare as a parking zone. The pork is thin and cooks in 3 to 4 minutes a side; it wants flame-kissed, slightly blackened edges. Work in batches and drop each batch straight into bowls of warm nước chấm — the pork is supposed to sit in the sauce. Corn and eggplant go on first while the fire climbs; bananas go on last over the fading coals.

Charcoal: 1.5 to 2 chimneys total. The cheapest menu in the book to fuel.

Bún Chả
Main · Serves 8 · 20 min at the grill

Two forms of pork over one fire: thin slices of belly, and small flattened patties of the same marinated mixture. Both go into a warm bowl of nước chấm, and the eater builds each mouthful with noodles and herbs.

  • 2½ lb skin-on pork belly, sliced ¼ inch thick
  • 2 lb ground pork
  • Bún chả marinade (Chapter 24), divided
  • Nước chấm, warm, with đồ chua (Chapter 22)
  • 2 lb rice vermicelli, soaked and drained
  • Herb platter: mint, Thai basil, perilla, cilantro, lettuce, bean sprouts
  1. Marinate the belly slices and the ground pork separately, overnight.
  2. Form the ground pork into 24 small patties about 2 inches across and ⅓ inch thick — thin, so they char before they dry.
  3. Over a hot direct fire: belly slices 3 to 4 minutes a side, patties 3 minutes a side. You want blackened edges and rendered fat. Work in batches.
  4. Drop each batch straight into bowls of warm nước chấm with pickles floating in them.
  5. Serve the noodles at room temperature and the herbs whole, on a platter. Each person dips noodles and herbs into their bowl and eats from it.
The bowl is the dish

Do not plate this. The pork sitting in warm, diluted, sweet-sour fish sauce, picking up smoke and fat, is the entire point of bún chả — it is closer to a soup you build yourself than to a plate of grilled meat.

Nem Nướng lemongrass pork sausage
Appetizer · Makes 16 skewers · 15 min
  • 1 lb ground pork (fatty — 25% fat or better)
  • 3 stalks lemongrass, tender core only, minced to a paste
  • 4 cloves garlic · 3 shallots, minced
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce · 2 tbsp sugar · 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp black pepper · 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • Lettuce, herbs and nước chấm, to serve
  1. Combine everything and beat hard in one direction for 2 minutes until the mixture turns sticky and pale — this gives nem nướng its characteristic springy bite.
  2. Chill 30 minutes, then mould around skewers into 4-inch sausages.
  3. Grill over direct heat, turning, 8 to 10 minutes, until browned and firm.
  4. Serve with lettuce leaves for wrapping, a pile of herbs, and nước chấm.
Scallion Oil, Grilled Corn and Eggplant
Appetizer and side · Serves 8
  • Mỡ hành (scallion oil)
  • 8 scallions, thinly sliced · ⅓ cup neutral oil · ½ tsp salt · 1 tsp sugar
  • Corn
  • 8 ears corn · 3 tbsp dried shrimp, toasted and crushed · crushed peanuts
  • Eggplant
  • 4 Asian eggplants · fish sauce, lime, chilli, cilantro
  1. Heat the oil until it shimmers, pour it over the sliced scallions with the salt and sugar, and stir. It should sizzle and go bright green.
  2. Grill the corn over direct heat 10 minutes, brushing with scallion oil in the last few, then scatter dried shrimp and peanuts.

Chuối Nướng grilled sticky-rice bananas
Dessert · Serves 8 · 30 min
  • 8 small bananas, peeled
  • 2 cups glutinous rice, soaked 4 hours and steamed
  • 1 can coconut milk, ¼ cup sugar, pinch of salt (for the rice)
  • Banana leaves, softened over the fire
  • To serve
  • 1 cup coconut cream, warmed with 2 tbsp sugar and a pinch of salt
  • Roasted peanuts, crushed · toasted sesame seeds
  1. Stir the coconut milk, sugar and salt through the hot steamed rice and let it cool until workable.
  2. Press a layer of rice around each banana, encasing it, then wrap tightly in a banana leaf and tie.
  3. Grill over the fading coals, turning, 12 to 15 minutes, until the leaf is charred and the rice inside has crisped against it.
  4. Unwrap, slice on the diagonal, and pour warm coconut cream over. Peanuts and sesame on top.
Shortcut

Skip the rice and the leaves: grill the bananas bare over the embers until blistered and soft, 3 minutes a side, then split and pour the coconut cream over. It is 80 percent as good for 10 percent of the work.

Cà Phê Sữa Đá & Chanh Muối
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Cà phê sữa đá · per glass
  • 3 tbsp coarsely ground Vietnamese coffee · 3 tbsp condensed milk · ice
  • Chanh muối · per glass
  • 1 salted preserved lime, muddled · 1 tsp sugar · soda water · ice
  1. Put the condensed milk in the bottom of the glass. Set the phin filter on top, add the coffee, tamp lightly, and pour in a little just-off-the-boil water to bloom before filling.
  2. Let it drip through — 4 to 5 minutes. If it runs in under two, the tamp was too loose. Stir hard and pour over a full glass of ice.
  3. For chanh muối, muddle the preserved lime with sugar in the bottom of a glass, fill with ice and top with soda. Salty, sour, and the best thing there is in real heat.
  4. Also pour: Bia Saigon or 333 — light lager is the only correct beer here; a dry sparkling Riesling or Grüner Veltliner if you want wine against the fish sauce.
