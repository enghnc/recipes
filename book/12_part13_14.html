Part Thirteen · Sandwiches

Gua Bao

Spring · Casual · Two kettles · Deflector

Taiwan's night-market sandwich: a folded steamed bun, a slab of pork belly braised until it collapses and then charred over coals, pickled mustard greens for sourness, and a heavy dust of crushed peanut and sugar. The bun is steamed on the kettle in a bamboo basket, which is a use for a grill almost nobody thinks of.

The menu

  • Taiwanese popcorn chicken · Smoked tea eggs
  • Gua bao — braised and charred pork belly in steamed folded buns
  • Pickled mustard greens · Peanut sugar · Chilli oil
  • Peanut ice cream rolls

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal. One chunk of oolong-soaked wood or plain oak for the tea eggs and nothing else — this is not a smoky menu.

Kettle A — braise, then steam. Deflector at 300°F for the belly braise, 2½ hours in a covered Dutch oven. Then the same kettle becomes a steamer: a wide pot of simmering water on the grate with a stacked bamboo basket on top, lid down, for the buns.

Kettle B — frying and charring. A Dutch oven of oil over direct heat for the popcorn chicken, then a bare hot grate to char the braised belly slabs at the end.

Charcoal: about 3 chimneys on A, 2 on B.

Smoked Tea Eggs
Appetizer · Makes 12 · Overnight

Normally simmered in spiced tea; here they take on a little smoke first, which is a small liberty and a clear improvement.

  • 12 eggs, boiled 7 minutes and cooled
  • 4 cups water · 3 tbsp black tea leaves
  • ⅓ cup light soy · 2 tbsp dark soy · 2 tbsp rock sugar
  • 3 star anise · 1 cinnamon stick · 1 tsp five-spice · 4 slices ginger
  1. Tap each cooled egg all over with the back of a spoon to craze the shell without removing it. The cracks are what make the marbled pattern.
  2. Set the cracked eggs on the indirect side at 250°F with one oak chunk for 20 minutes.
  3. Simmer the tea and aromatics 10 minutes, add the eggs, and steep overnight in the fridge in the liquid.
  4. Peel just before serving to show the marbling.
To the table · bowlPeeled and halved, marbled side up, in a shallow bowl with a little of the steeping liquid spooned over so they stay glossy. Room temperature. Put them out early — they are something to pick at while the belly chars.
Taiwanese Popcorn Chicken yán sū jī
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 25 min

Sweet potato starch is what makes the coating craggy and shatteringly crisp rather than smooth — cornstarch gives a completely different and lesser result.

  • 2 lb boneless chicken thigh, in 1-inch pieces
  • 3 tbsp light soy · 1 tbsp Shaoxing wine · 1 tsp five-spice
  • 1 tsp white pepper · 2 cloves garlic, grated · 1 tsp sugar · 1 egg
  • 1½ cups coarse sweet potato starch
  • 1 cup dried Thai basil leaves
  • Frying oil · white pepper and five-spice salt, to finish
  1. Marinate the chicken 1 to 4 hours in everything but the starch and basil.
  2. Toss the pieces in the starch and let them sit 5 minutes until the coating looks damp and clumpy. Those clumps are the craggy bits.
  3. Fry in a Dutch oven at 340°F for 3 minutes, then lift out and rest 3 minutes.
  4. Second fry at 375°F for 60 seconds. Throw the basil leaves in for the final 10 seconds — they crisp instantly and will spit, so stand back.
  5. Drain and toss with five-spice salt and white pepper while hot.
To the table · basketPaper-lined basket, basil scattered through it, wooden skewers or toothpicks stuck in. Eaten standing, with beer, within five minutes of frying. Never covered.
Gua Bao braised and charred pork belly
Main · Makes 16 · Braise ahead, 10 min to finish

The trick that makes this better than a restaurant version: braise the belly a day ahead, chill it solid in its own liquid, then slice and char it. Cold belly slices cleanly and takes a hard sear; warm belly falls apart.

  • 4 lb skin-on pork belly, in one slab
  • ⅓ cup light soy · 3 tbsp dark soy · ½ cup Shaoxing wine
  • ⅓ cup rock sugar · 4 slices ginger · 6 cloves garlic
  • 4 scallions · 3 star anise · 1 cinnamon stick · 1 tsp five-spice
  • Water to come halfway up
  • To serve
  • 16 steamed bao buns · pickled mustard greens
  • Peanut sugar: 1 cup roasted peanuts crushed coarse with ¼ cup caster sugar
  • Cilantro sprigs · chilli oil
  1. Brown the belly slab on all sides in a Dutch oven over direct heat.
  2. Add everything else, bring to a simmer, cover, and move to the indirect side at 300°F for 2½ hours, turning once, until a skewer meets no resistance.
  3. Cool the belly in its liquid and refrigerate overnight. This is the step that makes the dish work.
  4. Lift the cold slab out and cut into ½-inch slabs. Skim the fat off the braising liquid and reduce what remains to a glaze.
  5. Char the slices over a hot bare grate, 2 minutes a side, until the edges blacken and the fat begins to render again.
  6. Brush with the reduced glaze in the last 30 seconds.
  7. Build: open a warm bun, a slab of belly, pickled greens, a heavy spoon of peanut sugar, cilantro, chilli oil.
The peanut sugar

Not a garnish. Crushed peanut and sugar is what balances the fat and the sour pickle, and Taiwanese vendors apply it far more heavily than seems reasonable. Use a full tablespoon per bun.

To the table · communalWarm buns in the bamboo basket they steamed in, belly on a board beside it, and four bowls in a row: pickled greens, peanut sugar, cilantro, chilli oil. People build their own and always build a second. Keep the basket lid on between rounds.
Steamed Bao Buns
Side · Makes 16 · 3 hr
  • 4 cups bao flour or plain flour
  • 2 tsp instant yeast · 1 tsp baking powder
  • ¼ cup sugar · 1 tsp salt
  • ¾ cup warm milk · ½ cup warm water · 3 tbsp neutral oil
  1. Mix and knead 10 minutes to a smooth, firm, slightly dry dough. Rise 1 hour.
  2. Divide into 16, roll each into an oval about ⅛ inch thick, brush one half lightly with oil, and fold over — the oil is what stops the halves welding shut.
  3. Set each on a square of parchment and proof 45 minutes until visibly puffy.
  4. Set a wide pot of water on the grate over a hot fire and stack bamboo baskets on top. Close the kettle lid.
  5. Steam 10 to 12 minutes. Then turn the heat off and wait 3 minutes before lifting the lid — a sudden temperature drop makes bao wrinkle and collapse.
  6. They hold in the basket for an hour and re-steam in 3 minutes.
Pickled Mustard Greens
Side · Makes 3 cups · 3 days
  • 1 large head mustard greens, chopped
  • 3 tbsp salt · 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 cup rice vinegar · 2 cloves garlic · 2 dried chillies
  • To finish
  • 1 tbsp oil · 1 tsp sugar · 1 tsp soy
  1. Toss the greens with the salt and leave 2 hours, then squeeze hard.
  2. Pack into a jar with the sugar, vinegar, garlic and chillies. Refrigerate 3 days minimum.
  3. Before serving, squeeze dry, chop finely, and fry briefly in oil with a little sugar and soy. That quick fry is what turns pickle into a condiment.
  4. A jar of Taiwanese suan cai is a legitimate shortcut; still fry it before use.
Peanut Ice Cream Rolls
Dessert · Makes 8 · 10 min

A Yilan night-market classic and genuinely strange in the best way: peanut brittle shaved into snow, two scoops of ice cream, and a handful of fresh cilantro, rolled in a thin wrapper. The cilantro is not a mistake.

  • 8 thin spring-roll or popiah wrappers
  • 1 large block peanut brittle
  • 1 pint vanilla or taro ice cream
  • 1 bunch cilantro, leaves picked
  1. Shave the brittle with a box grater or a sharp knife into fine sweet shavings — like coarse sand, not chunks.
  2. Warm each wrapper for 10 seconds on a dry pan at the edge of the fire so it becomes pliable.
  3. Scatter a thick layer of shaved peanut over the wrapper, add two small scoops of ice cream, then a generous pinch of cilantro.
  4. Roll like a burrito and hand it over immediately. This has a ninety-second window.
To the table · handRolled to order and handed straight over — there is no plating and no waiting. Tell people about the cilantro before they bite, and make a second for the ones who doubted it.
Winter Melon Tea & What to Pour
Drinks · Serves 8
  • 1 block winter melon sugar (Asian grocer) · 8 cups water · lemon
  • Cold-brewed oolong, unsweetened
  • Taiwan Beer or any crisp lager · plum wine
  1. Dissolve the winter melon sugar in hot water, cool, and serve over ice with lemon. Amber, caramel-scented and unlike anything else.
  2. Cold-brew the oolong in the fridge for 6 hours. Serve unsweetened as the counterweight to the peanut sugar.
  3. Also pour: cold lager throughout, plum wine over ice with the dessert.

Bánh Mì

Summer · Casual lunch · One kettle · Pizza stone

The best sandwich in the world, and the bread is why. A Vietnamese baguette is not a French one — rice flour and a hotter, steamier bake give a shatteringly thin crust and an airy, almost hollow crumb that compresses to nothing when you bite it. Bake your own and the difference is not subtle.

The menu

  • Bò lá lốt — beef in betel leaf · Gỏi cuốn fresh rolls
  • Bánh mì thịt nướng on Vietnamese baguettes
  • Chicken liver pâté · Đồ chua (Chapter 22) · herbs, chilli, Maggi
  • Chè ba màu — three-colour dessert

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal. No smoke wood.

One kettle, in three phases. Stone first at 475°F for the baguettes, with a pan of water thrown onto the coals for steam. Then deflector at 325°F for the pâté in a water bath. Then pull everything and run a hot direct bed for the pork and the betel-leaf rolls, both of which take four minutes.

Charcoal: about 2.5 chimneys total, plus a reserve chimney for the final direct phase.

Vietnamese Baguettes bánh mì
Side · Makes 8 · 4 hr

The rice flour is what does it — it weakens the gluten just enough that the crumb stays open and cottony and the crust goes thin and brittle rather than chewy. A French baguette in this sandwich is too dense and fights the filling.

  • 4 cups bread flour · ½ cup rice flour
  • 2¼ tsp instant yeast · 2 tsp salt · 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1½ cups warm water · 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • ¼ of a crushed vitamin C tablet (optional; helps the rise and the crumb)
  1. Mix and knead 10 minutes to a soft, slightly slack dough. Rise 1½ hours, or overnight cold.
  2. Divide into 8 and shape into short, fat torpedoes about 7 inches long with sharply tapered ends.
  3. Proof 45 minutes. Slash each one deeply down the centre lengthwise — one long cut, not several diagonal ones.
  4. Stone preheated to 475°F. Slide the loaves on, throw half a cup of water onto the charcoal grate, and close the lid immediately.
  5. 18 to 22 minutes, until pale gold and very light for their size. They should sound hollow and feel almost empty.
  6. Cool completely. A warm bánh mì roll tears instead of compressing.
Bò Lá Lốt beef in betel leaf
Appetizer · Makes 30 · 15 min

Seasoned beef rolled in wild betel leaf and grilled until the leaf blackens and turns intensely aromatic. If you can find lá lốt at a Vietnamese grocer, buy twice what you need — everyone eats four.

  • 1 lb ground beef (fatty)
  • 30 lá lốt leaves, or shiso
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, minced to a paste · 4 cloves garlic · 2 shallots
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce · 1 tsp sugar · 1 tsp five-spice · 1 tsp black pepper
  • Nước chấm (Chapter 22) · crushed peanuts · scallion oil
  1. Mix the beef with the aromatics and seasoning and beat it for a minute so it holds together.
  2. Lay a leaf shiny side down, put a finger of beef across the base, and roll tightly into a cigar. Secure with a toothpick or thread several onto a skewer.
  3. Grill over hot direct heat, 2 minutes a side. The leaf should blacken at the edges — that char is the flavour.
  4. Serve immediately with nước chấm, crushed peanuts and a spoon of scallion oil.
To the table · platterStraight off the fire onto a platter, leaves still blistering, with a bowl of nước chấm and lettuce leaves for wrapping. They have a five-minute window before the leaf goes leathery, so serve them as the first thing while everything else waits.
Gỏi Cuốn fresh rolls
Appetizer · Makes 16 · 25 min, no cook
  • 16 rice paper wrappers
  • 8 oz rice vermicelli, cooked and cooled
  • 1 lb cooked shrimp, halved lengthwise, or leftover grilled pork
  • Lettuce, mint, cilantro, garlic chives
  • Peanut dipping sauce
  • ⅓ cup hoisin · 3 tbsp peanut butter · 2 tbsp water · 1 tsp rice vinegar · crushed peanuts
  1. Dip each wrapper in warm water for 2 seconds only — it should still feel stiff. It softens completely while you fill it, and a fully softened wrapper tears.
  2. Lay the shrimp halves cut side up in a row near the top so they show through the finished roll.
  3. Add lettuce, noodles and herbs at the bottom third, fold the sides in, and roll up tightly.
  4. Whisk the sauce and top with crushed peanuts.
To the table · platterCut each roll once on a steep diagonal and stand the halves cut-side up so the pink shrimp through the translucent wrapper shows — that stripe is the whole look. Cover with a damp cloth until served; they go hard and cloudy in dry air.
Bánh Mì Thịt Nướng
Main · Makes 8 · 15 min
  • 3 lb pork shoulder, sliced ¼ inch thick
  • 4 stalks lemongrass, minced · 6 cloves garlic · 4 shallots
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce · 2 tbsp sugar · 2 tbsp honey
  • 1 tbsp soy · 1 tbsp oyster sauce · 1 tsp five-spice · 1 tbsp black pepper · 2 tbsp oil
  • To build
  • 8 baguettes · pâté · mayonnaise · Maggi seasoning
  • Đồ chua · cucumber batons · cilantro sprigs · sliced chilli
  1. Marinate the pork overnight. Thin slices and a sugar-heavy marinade are what give the charred, slightly sticky edges.
  2. Grill over a hot direct fire, 2 to 3 minutes a side, in batches. Burnt edges are correct; pale pork is not.
  3. Split each baguette along one side, keeping it hinged, and pull out a little of the crumb to make room.
  4. Build in order and do not deviate: pâté on one side, mayonnaise on the other, then a shake of Maggi, then pork, then đồ chua, cucumber, cilantro, chilli.
  5. Press the sandwich closed firmly. A bánh mì should compress.
To the table · handBuilt to order in an assembly line and handed over wrapped in paper, cut once on the diagonal if you must. Never plated, never pre-made — the crust softens within ten minutes of the mayonnaise going on, which is why every shop builds them one at a time.
Chicken Liver Pâté
Side · Makes 2 cups · 48 hr ahead
  • 1 lb chicken livers, trimmed and soaked in milk 2 hours
  • 4 oz pork fat or butter · 3 shallots · 4 cloves garlic
  • 3 tbsp brandy · 1 tsp five-spice · 1 tsp white pepper · 1 tsp salt
  • 2 eggs · ¼ cup cream
  1. Soaking the livers in milk removes the bitterness. Drain and pat dry.
  2. Sauté the shallots and garlic in the fat, add the livers and sear 2 minutes, then flame with the brandy.
  3. Blend everything with the eggs, cream and seasoning until completely smooth, then pass through a sieve.
  4. Pour into a small terrine or loaf tin, cover with foil, and set it in a roasting tray with an inch of hot water.
  5. Bake on the indirect side at 325°F for 45 to 55 minutes, until set with a slight wobble at the centre.
  6. Cool, then refrigerate 48 hours before using. It is grainy on day one and silky on day three.
Chè Ba Màu three-colour dessert
Dessert · Serves 8 · Make ahead
  • 1 cup mung beans, soaked, steamed and lightly sweetened
  • 1 cup red beans, cooked with ⅓ cup sugar until syrupy
  • 1 cup pandan jelly strips (or green tapioca pearls)
  • 2 cans coconut milk warmed with ⅓ cup sugar and a pinch of salt
  • Crushed ice
  1. Cook each component separately and chill. All three can be made a day ahead.
  2. Layer into tall glasses in order: red beans, mung beans, pandan jelly.
  3. Pack crushed ice on top and pour the sweetened coconut milk over so it runs down through the layers.
  4. Serve with a long spoon and let people stir it into one colour themselves.
To the table · glassClear glasses, always — the three visible bands are the entire name and the entire point. Assemble at the last moment, hand them over unstirred, and let each person destroy their own.
Sinh Tố Bơ & Soda Chanh
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Sinh tố bơ — avocado shake
  • 3 ripe avocados · 1 cup condensed milk · 1 cup milk · 2 cups ice
  • Soda chanh
  • Juice of 6 limes · ½ cup sugar syrup · soda water · ice
  1. Blend the shake until completely smooth and thick enough to stand a straw in. Sweet avocado is standard across Vietnam and converts sceptics instantly.
  2. Soda chanh: syrup and lime in the glass, ice, then soda, stirred once.
  3. Also pour: Bia Saigon or 333 — cold, light and in bottles.

Turkish Kahvaltı

Spring · Long morning · Two kettles · Pizza stone

The greatest breakfast in the world and the one best suited to a grill: a table covered edge to edge in small dishes, two or three things cooked in cast iron over coals, bread off the stone, and tea that never stops being refilled. Kahvaltı means "before coffee" — the coffee comes at the very end, cooked in the embers.

The menu

  • The board — cheeses, olives, tomato, cucumber, kaymak and honey, jams
  • Menemen · Sucuklu yumurta · Çılbır
  • Simit off the stone · Muhammara
  • Turkish tea, then coffee in the embers

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal. No smoke wood — nothing here wants it.

Kettle A — the stone. 450°F for the simit, which take 15 minutes and can be baked an hour ahead. Then a direct corner for grilling bread and warming flatbread.

Kettle B — cast iron. A moderate direct bed running three pans in sequence: menemen, then sucuk and eggs, then the chilli butter for the çılbır. The çaydanlık — the double teapot — sits at the edge of the fire the entire time and is the real centre of the meal.

Charcoal: about 2 chimneys per kettle.

The Kahvaltı Board
The centre of the meal · Serves 8 · No cook

Not an appetizer — this is breakfast, and the cooked dishes are guests on a table that is already full. The rule is many small dishes rather than a few large ones.

  • Beyaz peynir, kaşar and tulum cheeses, cut into slabs and cubes
  • Green and black olives, dressed with oil and Aleppo pepper
  • 4 tomatoes and 3 cucumbers, sliced thick
  • Kaymak or clotted cream, with a pool of honey beside it
  • Apricot and sour-cherry jam · tahini stirred with pekmez
  • Butter, parsley, mint, lemon wedges
  1. Use as many separate small dishes as you own. Density and colour are the aesthetic; one large platter is the wrong shape entirely.
  2. Kaymak and honey go in one shallow dish side by side, never stirred together — you drag bread through both at once.
  3. Tahini and pekmez likewise: swirled but not combined.
  4. Everything at room temperature. Put it all out before anything hot appears, and leave it out for the whole three hours.
To the table · communalThe table should be covered so completely that there is nowhere to put a plate down — that is the target, not an exaggeration. Small dishes overlapping, bread in the gaps, tea glasses filling in the rest. Hot pans are set on top of whatever they will fit on.
Menemen
Main · Serves 8 · 20 min

Soft eggs folded through a stewed pepper and tomato base. The argument in Turkey is whether onion belongs; the argument everywhere else should be about not overcooking it.

  • 6 Turkish green peppers or 3 bell peppers, chopped
  • 1 onion, finely diced (optional and contested)
  • 6 ripe tomatoes, grated or peeled and chopped
  • 3 tbsp butter · 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 8 eggs, lightly beaten
  • 1 tsp Aleppo pepper · salt · parsley
  1. Soften the peppers in butter and oil in a large cast-iron pan over moderate direct heat, 8 minutes, without browning.
  2. Add the tomato and cook down 10 minutes until the liquid has gone and the mixture is thick and jammy. This is where menemen is usually rushed.
  3. Pour in the eggs and pull the pan to the cooler edge of the fire. Stir slowly and lift the mixture in folds.
  4. Take it off while it still looks slightly too wet — it carries on setting in the hot pan for a minute after it leaves the heat.
  5. Aleppo pepper and parsley over the top.
To the table · in the panIn the skillet, on a trivet, in the middle of the table, with bread — never portioned onto plates. People eat it straight from the pan with torn simit, which is also why it should be slightly underdone when it lands.
Sucuklu Yumurta
Main · Serves 8 · 10 min
  • 1 lb sucuk, sliced ¼ inch thick
  • 8 eggs
  • Aleppo pepper · black pepper · parsley
  1. Lay the sucuk in a cold cast-iron pan and set it over moderate heat. It renders a startling amount of deep red spiced fat — that fat is the dish and you should not pour any of it away.
  2. Cook 3 to 4 minutes a side until the edges curl and crisp.
  3. Crack the eggs straight into the pan between the slices. Cook until the whites set and the yolks are still liquid, 3 to 4 minutes, basting with the red fat.
  4. Aleppo pepper and parsley over the top.
To the table · in the panIn the pan, yolks intact, red fat pooled around the whites. Bread goes straight into the pan. Break the yolks at the table, not before.
Çılbır poached eggs on garlic yogurt
Main · Serves 8 · 15 min

An Ottoman dish that is roughly six hundred years old and looks like something from a modern restaurant. The yogurt must be at room temperature or the eggs go cold on contact.

  • 3 cups thick yogurt, at room temperature
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed to a paste with salt
  • 8 eggs · 2 tbsp white vinegar, for poaching
  • Chilli butter
  • 6 tbsp butter · 2 tsp Aleppo pepper · 1 tsp dried mint
  • Dill, to finish
  1. Beat the garlic paste through the yogurt and spread it across a wide shallow bowl in a swooping layer. Leave it out of the fridge for an hour first.
  2. Poach the eggs in barely-simmering water with the vinegar, 3 minutes for a liquid yolk. Lift out and drain well on a cloth.
  3. Set the eggs into the yogurt.
  4. Melt the butter in a small pan until it foams, take it off the heat, and stir in the Aleppo pepper and dried mint — off the heat, or the pepper burns and turns bitter in seconds.
  5. Pour the red butter over the eggs and yogurt. Dill last.
To the table · bowlOne wide shallow bowl for the table, not individual portions — the point is the red butter bleeding into the white yogurt, and eight small bowls destroy the picture. Pour the butter over at the table so people see it happen.
Simit
Bread · Makes 8 · 2½ hr

The Turkish street ring: dipped in grape molasses before it meets the sesame, which is why the crust goes dark and faintly sweet and why sesame sticks to it so completely.

  • 4 cups bread flour · 2¼ tsp instant yeast · 2 tsp salt · 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1⅓ cups warm water · 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Coating
  • ½ cup pekmez (grape molasses) mixed with 1 cup water
  • 2 cups sesame seeds
  1. Mix and knead 8 minutes. Rise 1 hour.
  2. Divide into 8. Roll each into a 26-inch rope, fold it in half, and twist the two strands around each other, then join the ends into a ring. The twist is what makes it a simit.
  3. Dip each ring fully in the diluted pekmez, let it drip, then press both sides hard into a tray of sesame seeds.
  4. Proof 30 minutes on parchment.
  5. Bake on the stone at 450°F for 15 to 18 minutes, until deep brown — much darker than you would take a plain bread.
To the table · basketStacked in a basket, torn by hand rather than cut, and used as the cutlery for everything else on the table. Make more than you think; simit is what people actually eat most of.
Muhammara
Side · Makes 2 cups · 20 min
  • 4 red peppers, charred in the embers and peeled
  • 1 cup walnuts, toasted
  • ½ cup breadcrumbs · 3 tbsp pomegranate molasses
  • 2 cloves garlic · 1 tbsp Aleppo pepper · 1 tsp cumin
  • ⅓ cup olive oil · lemon · salt
  1. Char the peppers directly in the coals until black, steam them covered for 10 minutes, then peel.
  2. Pulse everything to a coarse paste — it should stay visibly nutty and textured.
  3. Rest at least an hour. Finish with a pour of oil and a scatter of walnut pieces.
Çay and Coffee in the Embers
Drinks · Serves 8

Turkish tea is brewed in a stacked double pot and served dark or light to order by diluting from the top pot. Turkish coffee is traditionally cooked in hot sand; embers do the same job.

  • Çay
  • 4 tbsp Turkish black tea · a çaydanlık, or a small pot set over a larger one
  • Tulip glasses · sugar cubes
  • Turkish coffee
  • 1 heaped tsp very finely ground coffee per cup · 1 small cup of cold water per cup
  • Sugar to taste, added before brewing · cardamom, optional
  1. Tea: boil water in the lower pot. Put the leaves in the upper pot with a little of that water and let them steep over the steam for 15 to 20 minutes. Pour a third of a glass of the strong brew and top up with water from below — koyu for dark, açık for light. Keep it going all morning.
  2. Coffee: stir the coffee, sugar and cold water together in a cezve until dissolved. Settle it into a bed of hot embers, off to one side.
  3. Watch for the foam to rise up the sides. Lift it off before it boils, spoon a little foam into each cup, return the pot for a few seconds, then pour.
  4. Serve with a glass of water and something sweet. Let it settle a minute before drinking, and do not drink the last sip.
  5. Also pour: ayran — yogurt, water and salt, whisked to a froth — for anyone who wants it with the eggs.
