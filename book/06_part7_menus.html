Lunar New Year Duck

Winter · New Year · Two kettles · Rotisserie + pizza stone

Two ducks on the spit, lacquered mahogany, carved at the table into pancakes. The entire technique lives in what you do 48 hours before you light anything: scald, glaze, and air-dry the skin until it's tight and papery. Wet duck skin never crisps, and no amount of fire fixes it.

The menu

  • Smashed cucumber salad · Scallion pancakes
  • Rotisserie duck with mandarin pancakes, hoisin, scallion and cucumber
  • Charred gai lan · Smoky eggplant · rice
  • Egg tarts baked on the stone

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with 2 chunks of apple or pear wood. Keep the smoke very light — this is a roast, not barbecue.

Kettle A — the spit. Split baskets at 325°F with a drip pan between. Both ducks trussed tight, facing opposite directions. 1½ to 2 hours to 165°F in the thigh, fed 8 to 10 briquettes per side every 45 minutes. For the last 10 minutes, rake both baskets into one pile under the birds, lid off, and let the skin blister while it keeps turning.

Kettle B — stone, then deflector. Full chimney with the stone in from cold. Scallion pancakes and mandarin pancakes on the stone, then let the fire fall, slide the deflector in at 400°F, and bake the egg tarts. Gai lan, eggplant and rice over a direct corner.

Charcoal: 2.5 chimneys on A, 2 on B.

Smashed Cucumber Salad
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 15 min, no cook

Smashing rather than slicing tears the flesh into rough, craggy pieces that hold dressing in a way neat slices never do. Dress it within the hour or it floods.

  • 4 cucumbers
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 3 tbsp Chinkiang black vinegar · 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar · 1 tbsp sesame oil · 2 tbsp chilli oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, grated · toasted sesame seeds · cilantro
  1. Lay the cucumbers on a board and smash them with the flat of a heavy knife until they split lengthwise, then tear or chop into rough 1-inch pieces.
  2. Toss with the salt and leave in a sieve 15 minutes. Discard the water.
  3. Whisk the dressing, toss through, and serve within the hour.
Scallion Pancakes
Appetizer · Makes 6 · 40 min
  • 3 cups all-purpose flour · 1 tsp salt
  • 1 cup boiling water, ¼ cup cold water
  • ⅓ cup neutral oil mixed with 3 tbsp flour, as a paste
  • 6 bunches scallions, finely sliced · salt · white pepper
  1. Stir the boiling water into the flour with chopsticks, then the cold water. The hot water denatures some of the gluten and is what gives the pancake its tender, flaky layers. Knead 5 minutes, rest 30.
  2. Divide into 6. Roll each very thin, smear with the oil-flour paste, scatter scallions and salt.
  3. Roll up into a rope, then coil the rope into a snail and press flat. Roll out again to about 7 inches.
  4. Cook on the hot stone or in an oiled cast-iron pan, 2 to 3 minutes a side, until golden and blistered.
  5. Smash the hot pancake down on itself once or twice with tongs to loosen the layers, then cut into wedges.
Rotisserie Duck
Main · Serves 8 · 2 hr plus 48 hr drying
  • 2 whole ducks, 5–6 lb each
  • 1 tbsp five-spice · 1 tbsp salt, for the cavities
  • Scald and glaze
  • ⅓ cup maltose or honey
  • ⅓ cup rice vinegar · ¼ cup boiling water · 1 tbsp soy sauce
  • To serve
  • Mandarin pancakes · hoisin · scallions in batons · cucumber in batons
  1. Trim the excess neck and cavity fat. Season the cavities with five-spice and salt.
  2. Set each duck in a sink or over a bowl and pour about 2 quarts of boiling water over the skin, rotating, until it tightens visibly and turns opaque. Pat dry.
  3. Dissolve the maltose in the boiling water with the vinegar and soy, and brush the ducks all over.
  4. Set on racks, uncovered in the fridge for 24 to 48 hours, with a fan on them for the first few hours if you can. The skin should end up dry, tight and slightly translucent. This step is the dish.
  5. Truss tightly, spit both birds facing opposite ways, balance the load.
  6. Indirect at 325°F, drip pan below. 1½ to 2 hours to 165°F in the thigh.
  7. Consolidate the coals under the birds, lid off, 10 minutes to blister the skin.
  8. Rest 15 minutes. Carve the skin first, in squares, and serve it alone with pancakes — that is the first course. Then the breast, then the legs.
The drip pan

Duck renders an enormous quantity of fat — well over a cup per bird. Use a deep pan, check it at the halfway mark, and ladle some off if it's getting close to the rim. Keep it; it is the best roasting fat there is.

Mandarin Pancakes
Side · Makes 20 · 40 min

The trick is rolling them in pairs with oil between, so you get two paper-thin pancakes from one manageable disc and they peel apart after cooking.

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1¼ cups boiling water
  • Toasted sesame oil, for brushing
  1. Stir the boiling water into the flour, then knead 5 minutes to a smooth soft dough. Rest, covered, 30 minutes.
  2. Roll into a log and cut into 20 pieces. Flatten each into a small disc.
  3. Brush one disc with sesame oil and stack a second on top. Roll the pair together into a 7-inch circle.
  4. Cook on a dry stone or pan over moderate heat, 30 to 45 seconds a side, until just dry with a few pale spots. Do not brown them.
  5. Peel the two apart while hot and stack under a towel.
Charred Gai Lan & Smoky Eggplant
Sides · Serves 8
  • 2 lb gai lan, thick stems split lengthwise
  • 3 tbsp oyster sauce · 1 tbsp soy · 1 tsp sugar · 2 cloves garlic
  • Eggplant
  • 4 Chinese eggplants, halved lengthwise
  • 2 tbsp soy · 1 tbsp black vinegar · 1 tbsp chilli oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, grated · scallions · cilantro
  1. Toss the gai lan in a little oil and grill over direct heat, 3 to 4 minutes, turning, until the leaves char and the stems are just tender. Toss with the warmed sauce.
  2. Score the eggplant flesh in a crosshatch, oil it, and grill cut-side down 6 to 8 minutes, then flip and cook until collapsing.
  3. Dress hot with the soy, vinegar, chilli oil and garlic. Scatter scallions and cilantro.
Egg Tarts dan tat
Dessert · Makes 12 · 25 min
  • Pastry
  • 2 cups flour · ¼ cup powdered sugar · ½ tsp salt
  • 1 stick butter and 3 tbsp lard, both cold
  • 1 egg yolk · 3–4 tbsp ice water
  • Custard
  • ½ cup sugar dissolved in ¾ cup hot water, cooled
  • 4 eggs, beaten · ⅓ cup evaporated milk · ½ tsp vanilla
  1. Make the pastry as for any short crust: rub the fats in, bind with yolk and water, chill an hour.
  2. Roll thin and press into a 12-cup muffin tin or foil tart shells.
  3. Whisk the cooled sugar syrup into the eggs, then the milk and vanilla. Strain twice — this is what gives the custard its glassy, unblemished surface.
  4. Fill the shells to just below the rim.
  5. Bake on the preheated stone over a deflector at 400°F for 18 to 22 minutes, rotating halfway. They are done when the custard has just set and wobbles as one piece; a puffed, cracked top means they went too far.
  6. Best warm, within an hour.
Jasmine Tea, Chrysanthemum & Salted Plum Soda
Drinks · Serves 8
  • 3 tbsp jasmine tea leaves · hot water at about 185°F
  • 1 cup dried chrysanthemum · 8 cups water · rock sugar
  • 8 preserved salted plums · soda water · ice
  1. Brew the jasmine below boiling — water at a full boil scalds green tea and turns it bitter — and refill the pot through the meal.
  2. Steep the chrysanthemum in just-boiled water for 10 minutes, sweeten lightly with rock sugar, and chill.
  3. For the plum soda, drop a salted plum into a glass, muddle once, fill with ice and top with soda.
  4. Also pour: Oregon or Burgundian Pinot Noir — duck's classic partner, fruit and acid against fat; a Gewürztraminer works beautifully against hoisin; Tsingtao throughout.

Kettle Thanksgiving

Late autumn · Thanksgiving · Two kettles · Deflector + pizza stone

The best single argument for owning a kettle. A spatchcocked turkey cooks in under two hours over a deflector with better skin and a juicier breast than any oven produces — and it gets the bird out of your kitchen entirely on the one day of the year the oven is the bottleneck.

The menu

  • Cast-iron baked brie · Ember-roasted onion dip
  • Spatchcocked turkey with drip-pan gravy
  • Cornbread and sausage dressing · Charred Brussels sprouts · Smoked sweet potatoes · Raw cranberry relish
  • Maple-bourbon pecan pie

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: briquettes with 3 apple chunks and 1 cherry. Budget 25 percent extra below 40°F.

Kettle A — the turkey. Coals ringed, deflector in, drip pan with stock and onions beneath. 350°F, bird flat and skin-side up, 1½ to 2 hours. Pull at 160°F in the breast and 170–175°F in the thigh — on a flat bird those arrive close together, which is the whole point of spatchcocking.

Kettle B — everything else. Deflector at 375°F for the dressing, sweet potatoes on the indirect side for an hour, sprouts over a direct corner, and the pecan pie on the stone while the turkey rests.

Charcoal: 3 chimneys per kettle.

Ember-Roasted Onion Dip
Appetizer · Makes 3 cups · 1 hr

Onions buried in the coals go far past what a pan can do — the outer layers carbonise and the inside turns to sweet, smoky pulp.

  • 4 large onions, unpeeled
  • 1 cup sour cream · ½ cup crème fraîche · ¼ cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tbsp cider vinegar · 1 tsp Worcestershire
  • Chives · salt and plenty of black pepper
  1. Bury the whole unpeeled onions directly in the coals. 40 to 50 minutes, turning once, until the outsides are black and they give completely when squeezed.
  2. Cool, peel away the charred layers, and chop the soft interior finely. Keep a little of the darkest edge for flavour.
  3. Fold into the dairy with vinegar, Worcestershire, chives and heavy seasoning.
  4. Rest at least 2 hours.
Cast-Iron Baked Brie
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 15 min
  • 1 wheel brie, about 1 lb
  • ½ cup cranberries · 2 tbsp honey · 1 tbsp orange juice
  • 1 sprig rosemary · ¼ cup toasted pecans
  • Grilled bread
  1. Score the top rind in a crosshatch and set the wheel in a small cast-iron pan.
  2. Scatter the cranberries around it, drizzle honey and orange juice over, and tuck in the rosemary.
  3. Set on the indirect side at 375°F for 12 to 15 minutes, until the cheese slumps and the cranberries burst.
  4. Scatter pecans and serve in the pan with grilled bread.
Spatchcocked Turkey
Main · Serves 8 · 2 hr plus 48 hr brining
  • 1 turkey, 12–14 lb
  • Kosher salt, 1 tsp per pound
  • 1 stick butter, softened, with 2 tbsp chopped sage and thyme
  • Drip pan
  • 2 onions, quartered · 2 stalks celery · 2 cups stock
  • Gravy
  • Drip pan contents · 4 tbsp butter · ⅓ cup flour · stock to loosen
  1. Spatchcock: with heavy shears, cut up both sides of the backbone and remove it. Flip the bird breast-up and press hard on the breastbone with the heel of your hand until it cracks and the bird lies flat.
  2. Salt all over and under the breast skin. Leave uncovered on a rack in the fridge 24 to 48 hours. Do not rinse it off.
  3. Work the herb butter under the breast skin on the day.
  4. Deflector in, coals ringed, drip pan with onions, celery and stock underneath. Stabilise at 350°F.
  5. Turkey on, flat, skin-side up, with the legs toward the hotter part of the ring. Apple and cherry wood on the coals.
  6. 1½ to 2 hours. Rotate the bird 180° once at the halfway point.
  7. Pull at 160°F breast / 170–175°F thigh. Rest 25 minutes.
  8. For the gravy, strain the drip pan, skim most of the fat, and whisk it into a butter-and-flour roux cooked in a skillet on the grill until nut-brown. Loosen with stock, season hard.
Why spatchcock

A whole turkey is a design problem: the thighs need 175°F and the breast is ruined past 160°F, and in a whole bird the breast is closest to the heat. Flattening it puts both on roughly the same plane, cuts the cooking time nearly in half, and exposes every square inch of skin to the fire.

Cornbread and Sausage Dressing
Side · Serves 8 · 55 min
  • 1 pan cornbread, baked a day ahead and torn into chunks
  • 1 lb bulk sage sausage
  • 2 onions, 3 stalks celery, diced · 4 cloves garlic
  • 1 stick butter · 2 tbsp chopped sage · 1 tbsp thyme
  • 3 cups stock · 2 eggs, beaten · salt and pepper
  1. Let the cornbread go stale overnight, uncovered. Fresh cornbread turns to porridge.
  2. Brown the sausage in a Dutch oven over direct heat, then soften the onion, celery and garlic in the fat with the butter and herbs.
  3. Fold in the cornbread, then the stock and eggs. It should be wet but not soupy.
  4. Move to the indirect side at 375°F, lid on, 35 minutes. Then take the lid off — or shovel coals onto it — for a final 15 to 20 minutes to crisp the top.
Charred Brussels Sprouts, Smoked Sweet Potatoes & Cranberry Relish
Sides · Serves 8
  • 3 lb Brussels sprouts, halved · ½ lb bacon, chopped
  • 2 tbsp cider vinegar · 1 tbsp maple syrup
  • Sweet potatoes
  • 4 lb sweet potatoes, whole
  • 1 stick butter · 3 tbsp maple syrup · 2 tbsp bourbon · pinch of salt
  • Relish · raw, made ahead
  • 1 lb cranberries · 1 orange, quartered with peel · ¾ cup sugar · pinch of salt
  1. Render the bacon in a cast-iron pan, add the sprouts cut-side down in one layer, and leave them alone for 5 minutes to blacken before tossing. Finish with vinegar and maple.
  2. Set the whole sweet potatoes on the indirect side at 375°F for 55 to 70 minutes, until collapsing. Split and top with maple-bourbon butter.
  3. For the relish, pulse the cranberries, whole orange and sugar in a food processor to a coarse texture. Rest overnight. Raw relish is sharper, brighter and far better than the cooked kind.
Maple-Bourbon Pecan Pie
Dessert · Serves 8 · 50 min
  • ½ batch all-butter pie dough (Chapter 21), in a 9-inch foil pan
  • 3 eggs · ¾ cup maple syrup · ½ cup golden or corn syrup
  • ½ cup brown sugar · 4 tbsp melted butter
  • 3 tbsp bourbon · 1 tsp vanilla · ½ tsp salt
  • 2½ cups pecan halves
  1. Line the pan, crimp, and chill hard.
  2. Whisk the filling ingredients, then stir in the pecans and pour in.
  3. Bake on the preheated stone over the deflector at 375°F for 45 to 50 minutes, rotating halfway. The stone is what gives a pecan pie the crisp bottom it almost never gets in an oven.
  4. Done when the edges are set and the centre still has a slight wobble. Cool at least 2 hours before cutting — it sets as it cools.

Owensboro Mutton

Late summer · Church picnic · Two kettles · Deflector / snake

Western Kentucky barbecue — the one major American barbecue tradition almost nobody outside it has heard of. Mutton, heavy hickory smoke, and a thin black Worcestershire-and-vinegar mop called dip. Mutton takes smoke in a way no other meat does; where a pork shoulder would be overwhelmed, mutton absorbs it and comes back stronger.

The menu

  • Beer cheese · Grilled bologna on white bread
  • Barbecued mutton shoulder with black dip
  • Burgoo · Vinegar slaw · Skillet cornbread · Cucumber and onion in vinegar
  • Bourbon chess pie

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: briquettes for the snake plus hickory chunks, and be generous — five or six is not too many here.

Kettle A — the mutton. A 2×2 snake three-quarters around, hickory on the first half, water pan in the middle. 250–275°F for 8 to 10 hours to 200–205°F, mopped hourly from hour three. No wrap.

Kettle B — burgoo, cornbread, pie, bologna. Deflector at 325°F. Burgoo runs 4 to 5 hours and only improves. Cornbread in cast iron at 400°F. Chess pie on the stone at 350°F at the end.

Charcoal: 5 chimneys on A across the day, 3 on B.

Beer Cheese
Appetizer · Makes 4 cups · No cook
  • 1½ lb extra-sharp cheddar, grated by hand
  • ½ cup flat beer, at room temperature
  • 4 cloves garlic · 1 tbsp dry mustard
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire · 1 tsp hot sauce · ½ tsp cayenne
  • Celery, radishes and saltines, to serve
  1. Let the cheese come fully to room temperature — cold cheese will not emulsify and you'll end up with a grainy paste.
  2. Process everything, adding the beer slowly, until smooth and spreadable.
  3. Rest overnight. The garlic and cayenne both bloom considerably.
Grilled Bologna
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 10 min

Cut thick and scored, bologna behaves like a cheap steak — the cuts open up over the fire and the edges crisp. It is one of the great underrated grill foods of the American South.

  • 1½ lb bologna in one piece, cut into ½-inch slabs
  • 2 tbsp yellow mustard
  • White sandwich bread · dill pickles · more mustard
  1. Score each slab in a shallow crosshatch on both sides — this stops it doming into a bowl over the heat.
  2. Brush lightly with mustard and grill over direct heat, 3 to 4 minutes a side, until the cuts open and the edges blacken.
  3. Serve on soft white bread with pickle and mustard. Do not overthink it.
Barbecued Mutton with Black Dip
Main · Serves 8 with leftovers · 10 hr
  • 1 bone-in mutton shoulder, about 8 lb
  • 3 tbsp coarse salt · 2 tbsp black pepper · 1 tsp cayenne
  • Hickory chunks ×6
  • Black dip
  • 2 cups water · 1½ cups Worcestershire
  • 1 cup cider vinegar · ⅓ cup brown sugar
  • Juice of 2 lemons · 2 tbsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp salt · 1 tsp allspice · 1 tsp cayenne · 1 onion, halved
  1. Simmer all the dip ingredients in a pot on the grill for 20 minutes. It should be thin, dark and sharp — closer to a marinade than a barbecue sauce. Make it the night before.
  2. Rub the mutton with salt, pepper and cayenne. Refrigerate overnight.
  3. Build the snake with hickory on the first half, water pan in the middle. Stabilise at 250–275°F.
  4. Mutton on, fat side up. Leave it alone for three hours.
  5. From hour three, mop generously with dip every hour. The layers of Worcestershire and vinegar build a dark, tangy crust that is the signature of this style.
  6. Cook 8 to 10 hours to 200–205°F, adding coals as the snake burns down. No wrap.
  7. Rest 45 minutes, then chop with a heavy knife, bark and all. Toss with warm dip and pile onto soft buns with more dip poured over.
On mutton

Mutton is sheep over a year old, and it tastes strongly of itself — that is the point of this dish, not a flaw to be managed. If you can only get lamb, the method is the same and the cook is 2 hours shorter, but expect something milder. Goat is the closest widely available substitute.

Burgoo
Side · Serves 8 generously · 5 hr

A Kentucky stew cooked until nothing in it is identifiable any more. It is traditionally made in enormous batches at church picnics, it uses whatever meat is around, and it is considered improved by leftover barbecue stirred in at the end.

  • 2 lb chicken thighs, bone in · 1 lb mutton trimmings or stew meat
  • 2 onions, 3 stalks celery, diced · 6 cloves garlic
  • 4 lb potatoes, diced · 6 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 lb dried lima beans, soaked · 6 ears corn, kernels cut off
  • 1 lb okra, sliced
  • 8 cups stock or water · ¼ cup Worcestershire
  • 2 tbsp cider vinegar · cayenne · black pepper · salt
  1. Brown the meats in a Dutch oven over direct heat, then add the onion, celery and garlic.
  2. Add the stock, limas and tomato. Move to the indirect side at 325°F, lid on, and cook 2 hours.
  3. Pull the chicken out, strip the meat from the bones, and return it shredded.
  4. Add the potatoes and cook another hour, then the corn and okra for the final 45 minutes.
  5. Season hard with Worcestershire, vinegar, cayenne and pepper. It should be thick enough that a spoon stands briefly.
Skillet Cornbread, Vinegar Slaw & Cucumbers
Sides · Serves 8
  • Cornbread
  • 2 cups cornmeal · 1 cup flour · 2 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tbsp baking powder · 1 tsp baking soda · 1 tsp salt
  • 2 cups buttermilk · 2 eggs · 6 tbsp butter, plus more for the pan
  • Slaw and cucumbers
  • 1 green cabbage, shredded · ½ cup cider vinegar · 2 tbsp sugar · 1 tsp celery seed
  • 6 cucumbers and 2 onions, sliced thin · ¾ cup cider vinegar · 2 tbsp sugar · salt
  1. Preheat a buttered cast-iron skillet on the grill until it's genuinely hot before the batter goes in. That sizzle is what makes the crust.
  2. Whisk the dry ingredients, then the wet, then combine quickly. Pour into the hot skillet.
  3. Bake over the deflector at 400°F for 22 to 28 minutes, rotating halfway.
  4. Dress the slaw and the cucumbers at least 2 hours ahead. Both want to be sharp — they're cutting through mutton and Worcestershire.
Bourbon Chess Pie
Dessert · Serves 8 · 50 min

Sugar, butter, eggs and a spoonful of cornmeal, which is what separates a chess pie from a custard. The top cracks and caramelises; that's how you know it's done.

  • ½ batch all-butter pie dough (Chapter 21), in a 9-inch pan
  • 1½ cups sugar · 1 tbsp cornmeal · 1 tbsp flour
  • 4 eggs · 1 stick butter, melted
  • ¼ cup buttermilk · 3 tbsp bourbon
  • 1 tbsp cider vinegar · 1 tsp vanilla · ½ tsp salt
  1. Whisk the sugar, cornmeal and flour together first so the cornmeal disperses.
  2. Beat in the eggs, then the butter, buttermilk, bourbon, vinegar, vanilla and salt.
  3. Pour into the chilled shell and bake on the stone over a deflector at 350°F for 45 to 50 minutes, rotating halfway.
  4. Done when the top is deep gold and crackled and the centre barely wobbles. Cool completely — warm chess pie is soup.
Mint Julep & Peach Tea
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Julep · per glass
  • 2½ oz bourbon · ½ oz simple syrup · 8–10 mint leaves · crushed ice
  • Peach tea
  • 6 tea bags · 8 cups water · 4 ripe peaches, puréed · ½ cup sugar
  1. Julep: press the mint gently against the inside of the cup with the syrup — do not shred it, which releases bitterness. Fill with crushed ice, add bourbon, stir until the cup frosts, and top with more ice and a large mint bouquet placed where your nose will be.
  2. Peach tea: steep the tea 5 minutes, sweeten while hot, then stir in the peach purée and chill. Strain if you want it clear.
  3. Also pour: bourbon neat; a Kentucky common or malty amber ale; Ale-8-One if you can find it, and cold buttermilk, which is more traditional with mutton than it sounds.

Nordic Hunter's Table

Deep winter · Venison · Two kettles · Deflector + pizza stone

Midwinter, juniper, and a fire you stand close to. Venison loin is the leanest serious meat you can cook and the least forgiving — thirty seconds of inattention takes it from rare to grey. Everything else on this menu is designed to look after itself so you can watch the one thing that matters.

The menu

  • Gravlax on rye crispbread · Charred leeks with brown butter and hazelnut
  • Venison loin with lingonberry-juniper sauce · Game sausages
  • Hasselback potatoes · Buttered root vegetables · Pickled red cabbage
  • Kladdkaka with lingonberries and cream

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: briquettes, because it's cold, with birch if you can get it and oak if you can't. Throw a few juniper branches or a tablespoon of crushed juniper berries straight onto the coals in the last minutes of the venison — the aroma is the point, not the smoke.

Kettle A — the venison. A full chimney banked hard to one side. Sear the loin 90 seconds per side on four sides, then move to indirect with the lid on for 8 to 15 minutes. Pull at 128–130°F. Sausages over the cooler part of the direct zone.

Kettle B — stone, then deflector. Crispbread on the stone at 450°F first, then the deflector at 375°F for the potatoes and root vegetables, and the kladdkaka last at 350°F.

Charcoal: 2 chimneys on A, 3 on B with the cold allowance.

Gravlax
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 48 hr cure, no cook
  • 1½ lb skin-on salmon fillet, centre cut, pin bones out
  • ⅓ cup coarse salt · ⅓ cup sugar
  • 1 tbsp crushed white or black peppercorns
  • 1 large bunch dill, roughly chopped
  • 2 tbsp aquavit or vodka (optional)
  • Mustard-dill sauce
  • 3 tbsp Dijon · 1 tbsp sugar · 1 tbsp white wine vinegar
  • ⅓ cup neutral oil · 3 tbsp chopped dill
  1. Mix the salt, sugar and pepper. Lay half the dill in a dish, the salmon skin-side down on top, then the cure, then the rest of the dill. Sprinkle the spirits over.
  2. Cover, weight it with a couple of tins, and refrigerate 48 hours, turning once a day and pouring off the liquid that collects.
  3. Scrape off the cure, pat dry, and slice thin on a sharp angle, leaving the skin behind.
  4. For the sauce, whisk the mustard, sugar and vinegar, then drizzle in the oil until thick, and stir in the dill.
Rye Crispbread knäckebröd
Appetizer · Makes 4 large sheets · 25 min
  • 1½ cups rye flour · 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast · 1½ tsp salt · 1 tsp caraway
  • 1 cup warm water · 2 tbsp melted butter
  • Flaky salt
  1. Mix to a firm dough, knead briefly, and rest 45 minutes.
  2. Divide into 4 and roll each paper-thin — thinner than seems sensible — directly onto parchment.
  3. Prick all over with a fork and scatter flaky salt.

Venison Loin with Lingonberry-Juniper Sauce
Main · Serves 8 · 25 min

Venison has almost no intramuscular fat, which means it has no buffer. Cook it to the number and take it off — this is the one recipe in the book where a thermometer is not a convenience but a requirement.

  • 3 lb venison loin or backstrap, silverskin removed
  • 2 tbsp juniper berries, crushed
  • 2 tbsp coarse black pepper · 1 tbsp coarse salt
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil · 3 tbsp butter
  • Sauce
  • 1 cup game or beef stock · ½ cup red wine
  • ⅓ cup lingonberry preserves
  • 1 tsp crushed juniper · 1 shallot, minced
  • 2 tbsp cold butter · 1 tsp red wine vinegar
  1. Trim all silverskin — it does not render and it will make the meat curl.
  2. Bring the loin fully to room temperature, then coat with oil and press the juniper, pepper and salt into it.
  3. Sear over a hot direct fire, 90 seconds on each of four sides.
  4. Move to the indirect side, lid on, and cook 8 to 15 minutes to 128–130°F, checking early and often. Scatter juniper on the coals in the last few minutes.
  5. Rest 10 minutes under foil with a knob of butter on top, then slice into thick medallions.
  6. For the sauce, soften the shallot in a skillet, add the wine and reduce by half, then the stock, preserves and juniper. Reduce to a light syrup, then whisk in cold butter off the heat and sharpen with vinegar.

Kladdkaka Swedish sticky chocolate cake
Dessert · Serves 8 · 22 min

Deliberately underbaked in the middle — which makes it the single most forgiving cake you can put on a grill, because the failure mode of grill baking is undercooking and here that's the goal.

  • 1 stick butter, melted
  • 1¼ cups sugar · 2 eggs
  • ¾ cup all-purpose flour · ⅓ cup cocoa powder
  • 1 tsp vanilla · ½ tsp salt
  • 3 oz dark chocolate, chopped (optional, for pockets)
  • Whipped cream and lingonberry preserves, to serve
  1. Whisk the melted butter and sugar, then the eggs, then fold in the flour, cocoa, vanilla and salt. Do not beat air into it — kladdkaka should be dense.
  2. Pour into a buttered 9-inch cast-iron skillet and scatter the chopped chocolate over.
  3. Bake over the deflector at 350°F for 20 to 22 minutes, and no longer. The edges should be set and the centre still visibly wet when you jostle the pan.
  4. Cool 15 minutes. Serve with barely sweetened whipped cream and a spoonful of lingonberry.

Calçotada

Early spring · Rabbit and quail · Two kettles · Pizza stone

Catalonia's spring onion festival, which is less a meal than a controlled mess: charred calçots pulled out of the fire, peeled at the table, dunked in romesco and lowered into your mouth while wearing a bib. Then rabbit and quail over the same coals. It is the most fun menu in this collection and the least formal.

The menu

  • Calçots with romesco · Pa amb tomàquet
  • Conill a la brasa with allioli · Grilled quail · Botifarra
  • Escalivada · Mongetes
  • Crema catalana, burnt with a poker

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with vine cuttings (sarmientos) if you can get them, otherwise oak. Calçots want real flame, so start them early while the fire is still flaring rather than waiting for clean embers.

Kettle A — calçots, then rabbit. Start with a full chimney still flaming and lay the calçots directly on the coals. As the fire settles to steady embers, the rabbit goes on. Quail at the end, hot and fast.

Kettle B — escalivada, beans, bread. Bank the coals; peppers, eggplant and onions go into the embers. Beans in cast iron, bread over the direct side, and the stone if you want to bake the crema bases rather than setting them on the stovetop.

Charcoal: 2.5 chimneys on A, 2 on B.

Calçots
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 30 min

The blackening is not a mistake. The outer layers are sacrificed entirely; you slide the sweet steamed core out from inside the burnt shell, hold it above your head, and lower it in.

  • 4–5 lb calçots, large spring onions or small leeks, roots trimmed
  • Newspaper
  • Romesco, to serve
  1. Lay the onions in a tight bundle directly on flaming coals, all facing the same way.
  2. 8 to 12 minutes, turning once, until the outside is uniformly black and the inside gives when squeezed.
  3. Wrap the whole bundle in newspaper immediately and leave 15 minutes. They steam in their own heat and this is what makes the inside silky.
  4. To eat: grip the black outer layers near the top, pull the pale core out with the other hand, dunk it in romesco, and eat it from above. You will need a bib and you will not stay clean.
Romesco
Sauce · Makes 3 cups · Make ahead
  • 4 ñora or 2 dried sweet red peppers, soaked and scraped
  • 2 roasted red peppers · 4 ripe tomatoes, charred
  • 1 head garlic, roasted in the embers
  • ¾ cup blanched almonds and ½ cup hazelnuts, toasted
  • 1 slice country bread, fried or grilled
  • 3 tbsp sherry vinegar · 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • ¾ cup olive oil · salt
  1. Char the tomatoes and garlic in the embers until blackened and soft. Squeeze the garlic out of its skins.
  2. Grind the nuts and bread first, to a coarse meal — this gives romesco its body and stops it turning into a smoothie.
  3. Add everything else and pulse, drizzling the oil in, to a thick, slightly grainy sauce.
  4. Rest overnight. Serve at room temperature in bowls big enough to dunk a whole onion into.
Conill a la Brasa grilled rabbit
Main · Serves 8 · 45 min

Rabbit is lean and unforgiving in the other direction from venison: it wants to be cooked through, but a few degrees past done and it turns to sawdust. A short brine is the best insurance.

  • 3 rabbits, spatchcocked flat
  • Brine
  • 8 cups water · ½ cup salt · ¼ cup sugar · 4 bay leaves
  • Baste
  • ½ cup olive oil · 6 cloves garlic, crushed · thyme · lemon · black pepper
  • Allioli
  • 6 cloves garlic · 1 tsp salt · 1 egg yolk · 1½ cups olive oil · lemon
  1. Brine the rabbits 1 to 2 hours, then rinse and dry thoroughly.
  2. Grill bone-side down over moderate direct heat for 15 minutes, then flip and move to the cooler edge for 20 to 25 minutes, basting with the garlic oil every 5 minutes.
  3. Pull at 160°F in the thickest part of the thigh. Rest 10 minutes and cut into pieces.
  4. For the allioli, pound the garlic and salt to a smooth paste in a mortar, work in the yolk, then add the oil drop by drop at first, whisking constantly, until it emulsifies and thickens. Finish with lemon. If it splits, start with a fresh yolk and whisk the broken sauce into it slowly.
Grilled Quail & Botifarra
Main · Serves 8 · 15 min
  • 8 quail, spatchcocked
  • 3 tbsp olive oil · 2 tsp smoked paprika · thyme · lemon zest · salt
  • 1½ lb botifarra or mild pork sausage
  1. Rub the quail with oil, paprika, thyme, zest and salt an hour ahead.
  2. Grill skin-side down over direct heat 4 to 5 minutes, then flip for 3 to 4 minutes. Quail cooks fast and is best just past pink at the bone.
  3. Botifarra goes over moderate heat, turning often, 12 to 15 minutes to 160°F. Don't prick it.
Escalivada & Mongetes
Sides · Serves 8
  • 6 red peppers · 4 eggplants · 4 onions, unpeeled
  • ⅓ cup olive oil · 1 tbsp sherry vinegar · 2 cloves garlic · salt
  • Mongetes
  • 1 lb dried white beans, cooked until tender
  • ¼ cup olive oil · 6 cloves garlic, sliced · parsley · salt
  1. Escalivar means to cook in ashes. Bury the whole vegetables in the embers and cook until the skins are completely black and they have collapsed — peppers 20 minutes, eggplants 25 to 35, onions 40.
  2. Put everything in a covered bowl for 15 minutes, then peel. Tear the flesh into long strips with your fingers rather than cutting it.
  3. Dress with oil, vinegar, garlic and salt and serve at room temperature.
  4. For the beans, fry the sliced garlic in oil in a cast-iron pan until just gold, add the drained beans, and toss gently until hot. Parsley and salt at the end.
Crema Catalana
Dessert · Serves 8 · Make ahead

Lighter than a crème brûlée — milk rather than cream, thickened with cornstarch, scented with lemon and cinnamon. Traditionally the top is burnt with a red-hot iron disc, which is easy to approximate on a fire.

  • 4 cups whole milk
  • Peel of 1 lemon and 1 orange, in strips · 1 cinnamon stick
  • 8 egg yolks · ¾ cup sugar · ⅓ cup cornstarch
  • Extra sugar, for the tops
  1. Infuse the milk with the citrus peel and cinnamon over low heat for 20 minutes, then strain.
  2. Whisk the yolks, sugar and cornstarch, then temper in the hot milk.
  3. Return to the heat and cook, whisking constantly, until it thickens noticeably — 5 to 8 minutes. It must boil briefly for the starch to set.
  4. Pour into shallow dishes and chill at least 4 hours.
  5. To burn: heat a flat metal spatula or a clean steel disc in the coals until it glows. Sprinkle each custard with an even layer of sugar and press the hot metal down onto it for a second or two. Reheat between each. It caramelises instantly and smells extraordinary.
Hot metal

Use long tongs and heavy gloves, do this away from guests, and rest the hot implement on a fireproof surface between custards. A blowtorch does the same job with less drama.

Vermut, Cava & Orxata

Nochebuena Lechón

Winter · Christmas Eve · Two kettles · Rotisserie

Cuban Christmas Eve. Pork shoulder injected and marinated in sour orange mojo, turned on the spit until the skin shatters, carved into a pile and eaten past midnight with black beans and rice. Where the porchetta is herbal and the al pastor is chile and pineapple, this is citrus, garlic and oregano — the same animal, an entirely different meal.

The menu

  • Tostones with mojo · Croquetas de jamón · Yuca con mojo
  • Lechón asado
  • Congrí · Ensalada de aguacate · Plátanos maduros
  • Flan de coco and guava pastelitos

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with 2 chunks of guava wood if you can find it, otherwise oak or a little pecan. Light smoke — the mojo is the flavour.

Kettle A — the spit. Split baskets at 325°F, drip pan between. 4 to 5 hours to 195–200°F for pull-apart texture, fed 10 briquettes per side every 45 minutes. Then the crackling blast: coals raked into one pile under the roast, lid off, 10 to 15 minutes until the skin blisters and pops audibly.

Kettle B — frying, beans, stone. Two-zone. A Dutch oven with 3 inches of oil over the direct side for tostones and croquetas; congrí over the indirect side for 45 minutes; pastelitos on the stone at the end.

Charcoal: 4 chimneys on A, 2.5 on B.

Mojo Criollo
Marinade and sauce · Makes 4 cups · Make ahead

Sour orange is a bitter Seville-type citrus, not a sweet orange. If you can't find it, two parts orange juice to one part lime is the standard substitute and works well.

  • 2 cups sour orange juice (or 1⅓ cups orange + ⅔ cup lime)
  • 20 cloves garlic, crushed to a paste
  • 3 tbsp dried oregano · 2 tbsp ground cumin
  • 2 tbsp salt · 1 tbsp black pepper
  • 1 cup olive oil · 1 onion, thinly sliced
  1. Pound or blend the garlic with the salt into a paste — this is a garlic sauce with citrus in it, not the other way round.
  2. Whisk in the oregano, cumin, pepper and juice.
  3. Heat the oil until shimmering and pour it over the mixture with the sliced onion. It will sizzle and bloom.
  4. Divide: two-thirds for injecting and marinating, one-third held back — never in contact with raw meat — as the table sauce for yuca, tostones and the pork itself.
Lechón Asado
Main · Serves 8 with leftovers · 5 hr plus overnight
  • 1 bone-in, skin-on pork shoulder, about 8 lb
  • Mojo criollo, two-thirds of the batch
  • Coarse salt, for the skin
  • A meat injector
  1. Inject at least a cup of mojo deep into the shoulder at 2-inch intervals, in a grid. This is what separates a real lechón from a marinated exterior, and it is worth buying a $10 injector for.
  2. Rub the rest over and under every fold. Bag and refrigerate 24 hours.
  3. An hour before cooking, score the skin in a diamond pattern — through the skin, not into the meat — and salt it heavily.
  4. Truss, spit and balance. Indirect at 325°F, drip pan below.
  5. 4 to 5 hours to 195–200°F, feeding both baskets every 45 minutes.
  6. The crackling blast: pull the drip pan, rake all the coals into one pile directly beneath, lid off, and let it spin 10 to 15 minutes. The skin will blister, bubble and crack audibly.
  7. Rest 30 minutes, then chop the meat coarsely and break the crackling into shards over the top. Mojo at the table.
Tostones & Yuca con Mojo
Appetizer and side · Serves 8
  • 6 green plantains, peeled and cut into 1-inch rounds
  • Frying oil · salt · a bowl of salted water with crushed garlic
  • Yuca
  • 3 lb yuca, peeled and cut into batons
  • Mojo, warmed · 1 onion, sliced · lime
  1. Fry the plantain rounds at 325°F for 4 minutes, until pale gold and softened. Lift out.
  2. Smash each one flat with the bottom of a glass or a tostonera.
  3. Dip the flattened rounds briefly in the garlic water — this is the step most recipes leave out and it's what makes them crisp rather than leathery.
  4. Fry again at 375°F for 2 to 3 minutes, until deep gold and crunchy. Salt immediately.
  5. For the yuca, boil until a fork goes in easily — 25 to 35 minutes — then drain, char briefly on the grate, and drown in warm mojo with raw sliced onion.
Congrí & Plátanos Maduros
Sides · Serves 8

Black beans and rice cooked together in the bean liquor, which is what makes congrí different from beans served over rice and gives it its colour.

  • 1 lb dried black beans, soaked and simmered until tender, liquid reserved
  • 3 cups long-grain rice
  • ¼ cup olive oil · 4 oz bacon or salt pork, diced
  • 1 onion, 1 green pepper, diced · 8 cloves garlic
  • 1 tbsp cumin · 2 tsp oregano · 2 bay leaves · salt
  • Plantains
  • 6 very ripe (black) plantains, sliced on the diagonal · oil · salt
  1. Render the bacon in a Dutch oven over direct heat, then cook the onion, pepper and garlic until soft — the Cuban sofrito.
  2. Stir in the cumin, oregano and bay, then the drained beans, then the rice.
  3. Add 4½ cups of the reserved black bean liquid, topped up with water if short. Season well.
  4. Bring to a boil, cover, move to the indirect side, and cook 25 minutes undisturbed. Rest 10 minutes, then fork through.
  5. Grill the ripe plantain slices over direct heat, brushed with oil, 3 minutes a side, until black-edged and jammy. The riper and uglier the plantain, the better this is.
Flan de Coco & Guava Pastelitos
Dessert · Serves 8 · Flan made ahead
  • Flan
  • 1 cup sugar, for the caramel
  • 1 can condensed milk · 1 can evaporated milk · 1 can coconut milk
  • 5 eggs + 2 yolks · 1 tsp vanilla · pinch of salt
  • ½ cup shredded coconut, toasted
  • Pastelitos
  • 1 sheet frozen puff pastry, thawed
  • 8 oz guava paste, sliced · 4 oz cream cheese
  • 1 egg, beaten · coarse sugar
  1. Flan, a day ahead: melt the sugar in a dry pan until deep amber and pour it into the bottom of a cake tin. Blend the milks, eggs and vanilla, strain, and pour over. Bake in a water bath over the deflector at 325°F for 50 to 60 minutes, until set with a slight wobble. Chill overnight, then invert.
  2. Pastelitos: cut the pastry into squares, lay guava paste and a knob of cream cheese on half of each, fold and seal with a fork.
  3. Brush with egg, scatter coarse sugar, and bake on the preheated stone at 400°F for 15 to 18 minutes, until puffed and deep gold.
  4. Serve the pastelitos hot and the flan very cold, on the same plate.
Daiquiri, Cuba Libre & Cafecito
