Argentine Asado

Late spring · Casual family gathering · Two kettles · No accessories

An asado is not a barbecue with different seasoning. It is a different relationship with fire: you build a bed of embers, you salt the meat and nothing else, you put it on, and you turn it once. The heat is controlled by how many embers are under the grate, not by a vent or a timer, and the meal unfolds over three hours with people standing around the grill the whole time. Restraint is the technique.

The menu

  • Provoleta — a disc of aged provolone grilled in cast iron until molten
  • Choripán — grilled chorizo and morcilla on bread with chimichurri
  • Tira de asado y entraña — cross-cut short ribs and skirt steak
  • Chimichurri and salsa criolla (Chapter 22)
  • Ensalada mixta · Batatas a la parrilla
  • Duraznos a la parrilla — grilled peaches with dulce de leche

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: hardwood lump plus 3 or 4 oak chunks — the standard substitute for quebracho outside Argentina. Never pine.

Kettle A — the embers. Build a generous lump bed with oak chunks on top and let it burn down until the flames are gone and the coals are grey-edged. You want a moderate steady heat at the grate, not a sear — you should be able to hold a hand six inches above it for four or five seconds. Short ribs go on bone-side down and take 45 to 75 minutes. The skirt steak goes on at the very end, hot and fast.

Kettle B — everything else. Two-zone, medium. Chorizo and morcilla over direct; provoleta in a cast-iron skillet over direct; sweet potatoes on the indirect side; peaches at the end over the dying coals.

Charcoal: about 2 chimneys per kettle across the session, roughly 10–12 lb each, plus the oak.

Provoleta
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 15 min

The trick is a thick disc of a cheese dry enough to hold its shape. Aged provolone works; fresh provolone will simply melt into liquid and escape the pan.

  • 2 lb aged provolone, cut into two 1½-inch discs
  • 2 tbsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp red pepper flakes
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • Grilled bread, to serve
  1. Leave the cheese uncovered in the fridge for a few hours, or overnight — a dry surface browns instead of sticking.
  2. Preheat a cast-iron skillet over direct heat until a drop of water dances.
  3. Oil the pan, lay in a disc, and press the oregano and chilli onto the top.
  4. Grill 3 to 4 minutes until the underside is deeply browned and crusted, then flip.
  5. Another 2 to 3 minutes, until the centre is visibly molten but the disc still holds together. Serve immediately, in the pan, with bread — it turns to rubber as it cools.
Choripán
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 25 min
  • 8 chorizo links
  • 4 morcilla (blood sausage) links
  • 2 baguettes, cut into 4-inch lengths and split
  • Chimichurri (Chapter 22)
  1. Prick the chorizo once or twice. Grill over moderate direct heat, turning often — high heat splits the casings and loses the fat.
  2. 12 to 15 minutes, until firm and well browned, 160°F inside. Morcilla is already cooked: 5 minutes, just to crisp.
  3. Split each sausage lengthwise (mariposa, butterflied) and lay it in the bread.
  4. Toast the bread cut-side down for 30 seconds. Spoon chimichurri over generously and hand them out as they're made.
Tira de Asado cross-cut short ribs
Main · Serves 8 · 45–75 min

Salt is the only seasoning, and that is not an omission. Ask the butcher for flanken-cut ribs about ½ to ¾ inch thick across the bones.

  • 5–6 lb beef short ribs, flanken cut
  • Coarse sea salt
  1. Salt generously on both sides an hour before cooking, or lightly the night before.
  2. Lay the ribs bone-side down on a grate over moderate embers. The bones act as a heat shield for the meat above.
  3. Cook undisturbed 40 to 55 minutes. The meat should render slowly and the fat should drip without flaring. If flames appear, spread the embers out — you have too much heat.
  4. Flip and give the meat side 8 to 12 minutes to brown.
  5. Rest 10 minutes, then cut between the bones into strips.
On doneness

Argentine short rib is cooked well past medium — it should be tender and chewable rather than pink. Around 195°F the connective tissue softens and the strips pull apart easily.

Entraña skirt steak
Main · Serves 8 · 10 min
  • 3 lb outside skirt steak, membrane removed
  • Coarse sea salt
  1. Salt just before it goes on. Rake the embers into a hotter pile — this is the one piece of the asado that wants real heat.
  2. 3 to 4 minutes per side for jugoso (medium rare), pulling at 130°F.
  3. Rest 8 minutes, then slice across the grain into finger-width strips. Skirt cut with the grain is inedible; cut across it, it's the best thing on the table.
Ensalada Mixta & Batatas a la Parrilla
Sides · Serves 8
  • Salad
  • 2 heads lettuce, torn
  • 4 tomatoes, wedged
  • 1 red onion, sliced paper-thin and soaked 10 min in cold water
  • Olive oil, red wine vinegar, salt
  • Sweet potatoes
  • 4 sweet potatoes, cut into ¾-inch planks
  • Olive oil, coarse salt
  • 4 bell peppers, halved and seeded
  1. The salad is dressed at the table, in the Argentine way: oil, then vinegar, then salt, tossed by whoever is nearest.
  2. Toss the sweet potato planks in oil and salt. Grill on the indirect side of Kettle B, lid down, 20 to 25 minutes, turning once, until a knife slides through.
  3. Move them over direct heat for a final 2 minutes a side to char.
  4. Grill the peppers cut-side up on the indirect side for 15 minutes, then flip onto the coals side for 3 minutes to blister.
Duraznos a la Parrilla grilled peaches
Dessert · Serves 8 · 12 min
  • 8 firm-ripe peaches, halved and stoned
  • 2 tbsp melted butter
  • 1 cup dulce de leche
  • 1 cup crumbled butter cookies
  • Flaky salt
  1. Brush the cut faces with butter. Grill cut-side down over the fading embers, 4 to 5 minutes, until dark grill marks form and the fruit softens.
  2. Flip and give them 3 minutes on the skin side.
  3. Fill each hollow with a spoonful of dulce de leche, scatter crumbled cookies and a few flakes of salt, and serve warm.
Fernet con Coca & Limonada
Drinks · Serves 8

Fernet and Coke is the drink of the asado, and the ratio matters more than the brand. The official pour is 1 part Fernet to 3 parts Coke; Córdoba pours it considerably stronger.

  • Fernet con Coca · per glass
  • 50 ml Fernet Branca
  • 150 ml Coca-Cola, very cold
  • Plenty of ice
  • Limonada · serves 8
  • 1½ cups lemon juice (about 10 lemons)
  • 1 cup sugar dissolved in 1 cup hot water
  • 6 cups cold water, mint, ice
  1. Fill a tall glass with ice, pour the Fernet, then top with Coke poured down the side so it doesn't foam over. Do not stir hard.
  2. For the limonada, combine everything, chill, and bruise a handful of mint into the jug before serving.
  3. Also pour: Mendoza Malbec with the beef, a Torrontés as aperitif.

Fourth of July Low & Slow

Summer · Independence Day · Two kettles · Deflector, snake, vortex

The all-day American cook, and the purest demonstration of what a kettle can do. One grill runs a snake under a brisket for six hours without being touched; the other runs ribs, converts to a vortex for wings, and finishes by baking a cobbler. If you cook only one menu from this book to learn the equipment, cook this one.

The menu

  • Vortex wings · Pimento cheese with crackers
  • Snake-method brisket · St. Louis ribs, 3-2-1
  • Smoked baked beans · Buttermilk slaw · Grilled corn
  • Dutch-oven peach cobbler

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: Kingsford or similar briquettes for both snakes — uniformity is the whole point. Oak and hickory chunks for smoke, plus one cherry chunk for colour.

Kettle A — the smoker. A 2×2 briquette snake three-quarters around the perimeter, about 60 briquettes, wood chunks on the first third, water pan in the middle. Light 8–12 briquettes and set them at one end. Hold 250–275°F for six hours without refuelling.

Kettle B — ribs, then wings, then dessert. A second snake or a banked deflector fire at 275°F for the ribs. Once they're off, dump the vortex in and run wings at 400–450°F. Then drop back to 350°F for the beans and the cobbler.

Charcoal: 15–18 lb of briquettes across both kettles, plus a full reserve chimney and 5 or 6 wood chunks.

Vortex Wings
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 45 min

The dusting is what makes the skin shatter. Baking powder raises the skin's pH and helps it brown and blister; cornstarch dries it. Either works, and a mix of both works best.

  • 4 lb chicken wings, split
  • 1 tbsp baking powder (aluminium-free)
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • 2 tsp salt, 1 tsp black pepper, 1 tsp granulated garlic
  • Sauce of choice, warmed
  1. Pat the wings completely dry. Toss with the baking powder, cornstarch and seasoning until evenly dusted.
  2. Rest them uncovered on a rack in the fridge for an hour if you have it.
  3. Fill the vortex with charcoal, set it in the centre of the grate, and pour a chimney of lit coals over it. Target 400–450°F.
  4. Arrange the wings in a ring around the outer edge, not over the cone.
  5. Lid on, top vent over the wings. Rotate the lid 45° every 10 to 15 minutes so the hot draw moves around the circle.
  6. 30 to 40 minutes, until deep golden and 185°F — wings are better taken well past 165, which renders the fat under the skin.
  7. Toss in sauce off the heat, or serve naked with sauce alongside.
Pimento Cheese
Appetizer · Makes 4 cups · No cook
  • 1 lb sharp cheddar, coarsely grated by hand
  • 4 oz cream cheese, softened
  • ½ cup mayonnaise
  • 1 jar (4 oz) diced pimentos, drained
  • 1 tsp granulated onion, ½ tsp cayenne, black pepper
  • 1 tsp cider vinegar
  1. Grate the cheese yourself. Pre-shredded cheese is coated in starch and makes a gritty dip.
  2. Beat the cream cheese and mayonnaise smooth, then fold in everything else. Leave it lumpy.
  3. Rest at least 4 hours, preferably overnight.
Snake-Method Brisket
Main · Serves 8 with leftovers · 10–14 hr including rest
  • 1 whole packer brisket, 12–14 lb
  • ½ cup coarse black pepper
  • ½ cup coarse kosher salt
  • 2 tbsp granulated garlic
  • 2 tbsp yellow mustard, as a binder
  • Spritz: 1 cup apple juice + ½ cup cider vinegar + ½ cup water
  1. Trim the fat cap to about ¼ inch and square off any thin, ragged edges — they burn. Round the corners.
  2. Smear with mustard, then coat heavily with the salt-and-pepper rub. Refrigerate uncovered overnight.
  3. Build the snake, wood on the first third, water pan in the middle. Light 10 briquettes and set them at one end. Stabilise at 250–275°F.
  4. Brisket on, fat cap up, thick point end toward the burning end of the snake. Lid on, top vent opposite the fire. Do not open for 3 hours.
  5. Spritz every 45 minutes after hour three, only if the bark looks dry.
  6. At 150–165°F the temperature will stall. Wrap tightly in butcher paper (better bark) or foil (faster, more tender) and return it.
  7. Pull at 203°F, and only when a probe slides into the flat with no resistance. Temperature alone is not enough; feel decides.
  8. Rest wrapped, in towels, in a closed cooler, for 1 to 2 hours.
  9. Separate the point from the flat. Slice the flat pencil-thick across the grain; cube the point for burnt ends or slice it thicker. The grain runs in different directions in the two muscles — find it before you cut.
Budget the time

Roughly 1¼ to 1½ hours per pound at 250°F, so a 13 lb brisket is a 16-hour day including the rest. The rest is your buffer: finish early, hold it in the cooler, and you are never late.

St. Louis Ribs, 3-2-1
Main · 3 racks · 6 hr
  • 3 racks St. Louis-cut spare ribs
  • Sweet Pork Rib Rub (Chapter 23)
  • 2 tbsp yellow mustard
  • ½ cup apple juice, 4 tbsp butter, ¼ cup brown sugar for wrapping
  • 1 cup BBQ sauce
  1. Remove the membrane from the bone side — lift a corner with a knife, grip with a paper towel, and pull it off in one sheet. Ribs left with the membrane on never get tender in that layer.
  2. Mustard, then rub. Rest 1 hour.
  3. 3 hours bone-side down at 275°F, unwrapped.
  4. 2 hours wrapped in foil, meat-side down, with the apple juice, butter and brown sugar sealed in.
  5. 1 hour unwrapped, sauced in two thin coats, to set the glaze.
  6. Check with the bend test: lift a rack a third of the way along with tongs. It should bend to roughly 90 degrees and the surface should crack. Rest 10 minutes and cut between the bones.
Smoked Baked Beans · Buttermilk Slaw · Grilled Corn
Sides · Serves 8
  • Beans
  • 2 cans pinto and 2 cans navy beans, drained
  • 1 onion, diced · ½ cup BBQ sauce · ¼ cup molasses
  • ¼ cup brown sugar · 2 tbsp cider vinegar · 1 tbsp mustard
  • Brisket trimmings or 4 rashers bacon, chopped
  • Slaw
  • 1 green cabbage and 4 carrots, shredded
  • ½ cup mayonnaise · ½ cup buttermilk · 3 tbsp cider vinegar
  • 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp celery seed, salt and pepper
  • Corn
  • 8 ears, husked · butter, salt
  1. Combine the bean ingredients in a cast-iron skillet and set it on the indirect side at 275°F, uncovered, for 2 to 3 hours. Sitting under the meat, catching drippings, is traditional and correct.
  2. Whisk the slaw dressing and toss with the vegetables at least 2 hours ahead, no more than 8.
  3. Grill the corn over direct heat, turning, 8 to 10 minutes, until charred in patches. Butter and salt immediately.
Dutch-Oven Peach Cobbler
Dessert · Serves 8 · 50 min
  • 5 lb peaches, sliced (about 10 cups)
  • ¾ cup sugar, 2 tbsp cornstarch
  • 1 tsp cinnamon, 1 tsp vanilla, juice of 1 lemon
  • Grunt & cobbler batter (Chapter 21)
  • 2 tbsp coarse sugar for the top
  1. Toss the peaches with sugar, cornstarch, cinnamon, vanilla and lemon. Tip into a buttered Dutch oven.
  2. Set on the indirect side at 350°F, lid on, until the fruit is bubbling — about 15 minutes.
  3. Drop the batter over the top in eight spoonfuls and scatter the coarse sugar.
  4. Lid on, and shovel 10 to 12 lit coals onto the lid for top heat.
  5. Bake 35 to 45 minutes, rotating pot and lid in opposite directions every 15 minutes, until the topping is golden and a skewer comes out clean.
Sweet Tea & Bourbon-Peach Smash
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Sweet tea
  • 6 black tea bags · 8 cups water · ¾ cup sugar · lemon
  • Bourbon-peach smash · per glass
  • 2 oz bourbon · ½ ripe peach, muddled
  • ¾ oz lemon juice · ½ oz simple syrup · mint
  1. Steep the tea 5 minutes in just-boiled water, no longer — over-steeped tea turns bitter and no amount of sugar hides it. Stir the sugar into the hot tea, then dilute and chill.
  2. For the smash, muddle the peach with lemon and syrup, add bourbon and ice, shake, and pour unstrained over crushed ice with a mint sprig.
  3. Also pour: cold American lager, or a hoppy pale ale against the fat.

Yakitori & Robata

Summer · Birthday or casual izakaya night · Two kettles · No accessories

An izakaya evening: skewers cooked over a narrow channel of intensely hot coals, glazed at the last moment, and served in waves as they come off. Smoke is kept to almost nothing here — Japanese grilling is built around tasting the ingredient, and heavy wood smoke would be a fault rather than a feature.

The menu

  • Edamame in a foil packet · Grilled shishito peppers
  • Negima (chicken and scallion) · Tsukune (chicken meatballs) · Miso-glazed saba
  • Shiitake and asparagus skewers · Sunomono · Rice
  • Grilled pound cake with matcha cream

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: hardwood lump, or binchotan if you can find it. No smoke wood.

Kettle A — the robata channel. Don't spread the coals. Pile lump into a narrow, deep strip down the centre of the charcoal grate so you have a band of fierce heat about four inches wide. Rest the skewers across the rim of the kettle with the meat over the channel and the handles out in open air. Cook lid off, 3 to 4 minutes a side.

Kettle B — fish, rice, vegetables, dessert. Two-zone. Miso fish skin-side down over direct then finished indirect; rice in a covered Dutch oven on the indirect side; pound cake over direct at the very end.

Charcoal: about 1.5 chimneys per kettle — lump burns hot and fast, so keep a reserve chimney lit from the start.

Edamame & Shishito
Appetizers · Serves 8 · 15 min
  • 1 lb edamame in the pod, 2 tbsp water, flaky salt
  • 1 lb shishito peppers, 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • Lemon, flaky salt, shichimi togarashi
  1. Seal the edamame in a heavy foil packet with the water and a big pinch of salt. On the grate over direct heat, 8 to 10 minutes, shaking once. Open, toss with more salt.
  2. Toss the shishitos in oil and grill directly on the grate, turning, 4 to 6 minutes, until blistered and slumped.
  3. Salt, squeeze lemon, dust with shichimi. Roughly one in ten shishitos is fiercely hot; this is part of the arrangement.
Negima chicken and scallion skewers
Main · Makes 24 skewers · 20 min
  • 4 lb boneless skinless chicken thigh, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 3 bunches thick scallions or negi, white and pale green parts, cut into 1-inch lengths
  • Tare (Chapter 22)
  • Salt, shichimi togarashi
  1. Thread alternately: chicken, scallion, chicken, scallion, chicken — five pieces per skewer, pressed together but not crushed.
  2. Season lightly with salt. Grill over the robata channel, 3 to 4 minutes per side, turning two or three times.
  3. In the last 2 minutes, brush with tare, let it set, brush again. Two or three coats.
  4. Off the heat, a final light brush and a dusting of shichimi. Serve immediately — yakitori does not hold.
Salt or tare

Yakitori is ordered either shio (salt only) or tare (glazed). Make half of each. Salt lets the chicken and the charcoal speak; tare is sweeter and more immediately pleasing. Most people order both.

Tsukune chicken meatball skewers
Main · Makes 16 · 25 min

The texture should be springy and slightly bouncy rather than crumbly. Beating the mixture hard in one direction develops the protein and gets you there; panko and a little cornstarch hold it on the skewer.

  • 1½ lb ground chicken (thigh, not breast)
  • ½ cup panko
  • 1 tbsp cornstarch
  • 3 scallions, finely minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tbsp sake, 1 tsp sesame oil, 1 tsp salt
  • Tare, to glaze · egg yolks for dipping, optional
  1. Combine everything and beat hard with a spoon or your hand for 2 to 3 minutes, always in the same direction, until the mixture turns tacky and pale and holds its shape.
  2. Chill 30 minutes — cold mixture is far easier to shape.
  3. With wet hands, form into 16 ovals and mould each around a flat skewer, squeezing gently so it grips.
  4. Grill over moderate heat first, 4 minutes per side, so the inside cooks before the outside colours.
  5. Move to the hot channel, glaze with tare in two or three coats, and char the edges. Pull at 165°F.
  6. Serve, if you like, with a small bowl of raw egg yolk for dipping — the traditional accompaniment.
Miso-Glazed Saba
Main · Serves 8 · 20 min plus marinating
  • 3 lb saba mackerel fillets or black cod
  • ½ cup white miso
  • 3 tbsp mirin, 3 tbsp sake, 2 tbsp sugar
  • Salt · lemon wedges · grated daikon, to serve
  1. Salt the fillets lightly and leave them uncovered in the fridge for an hour; blot off the moisture that beads up.
  2. Whisk the miso, mirin, sake and sugar. Coat the fish and marinate 4 to 24 hours — mackerel at the shorter end, black cod at the longer.
  3. Wipe off almost all the marinade. Miso is full of sugar and protein and will burn black in seconds if left thick.
  4. Grill skin-side down over direct heat, 4 to 5 minutes, without moving it, then move to the indirect side and finish 3 to 5 minutes more.
  5. Serve with lemon and a mound of grated daikon.
Vegetable Skewers · Sunomono · Rice
Sides · Serves 8
  • Skewers
  • 1 lb shiitake, stemmed · 2 bunches asparagus, trimmed
  • Neutral oil, salt, tare
  • Sunomono
  • 3 cucumbers, sliced paper-thin
  • ⅓ cup rice vinegar, 2 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp salt, 1 tsp soy
  • Toasted sesame seeds
  • Rice
  • 3 cups short-grain rice, rinsed until the water runs clear
  • 3⅓ cups water
  1. Skewer the shiitake through the caps and bundle the asparagus on two parallel skewers. Oil, salt, grill 3 minutes a side, glaze lightly with tare.
  2. Salt the cucumber, rest 10 minutes, squeeze dry, then dress with the vinegar mixture. Sesame on top. Best made a few hours ahead.
  3. For the rice, soak 30 minutes, then bring to a boil in a covered Dutch oven over direct heat. Move to the indirect side and cook, lid on and undisturbed, 15 minutes. Off the heat, rest 10 minutes, then fluff.
Grilled Pound Cake with Matcha Cream
Dessert · Serves 8 · 10 min
  • 1 pound cake, cut into 8 thick slices
  • 3 tbsp melted butter
  • 1½ cups heavy cream
  • 3 tbsp sugar, 1½ tsp matcha powder, sifted
  • Toasted sesame seeds or a little flaky salt
  1. Whisk the matcha with the sugar first, then whip into the cream to soft peaks. Sifting matters — matcha clumps and the lumps are bitter.
  2. Brush the cake slices with butter and grill over the fading coals, 60 to 90 seconds a side, until striped and crisp at the edges.
  3. Serve warm with a spoonful of the cream and a scatter of sesame.
Highball & Cold Barley Tea
Drinks · Serves 8
  • Whisky highball · per glass
  • 1½ oz Japanese whisky · 4½ oz soda water, very cold · a large clear ice cube · lemon peel
  • Mugicha · serves 8
  • 8 roasted barley tea bags · 8 cups cold water
  1. Chill the glass and the whisky. Fill with ice, pour the whisky, add the soda in one smooth pour down a bar spoon, and stir exactly once — the bubbles are the drink.
  2. Cold-brew the barley tea in the fridge for 4 hours. Serve unsweetened and very cold; it is the standard Japanese summer drink and cuts grilled food beautifully.
  3. Also pour: chilled junmai sake, or Asahi and Sapporo. Calpico soda and ramune for anyone not drinking.

Aegean Seafood

Summer · Elegant dinner party · Two kettles · No accessories

A psarotaverna by the sea. Whole fish dressed in nothing but olive oil, lemon, red wine vinegar and oregano; octopus braised soft and then charred hard; lamb souvlaki turning over the second grill. Meze arrive continuously from the moment people sit down, which is what makes this the least stressful of the summer menus.

The menu

  • Grilled octopus · Grilled halloumi
  • Whole grilled branzino · Lamb souvlaki
  • Horiatiki · Tzatziki (Chapter 22) · Pita (Chapter 20) · Grilled courgette and peppers
  • Grilled figs and peaches with honey and yogurt

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: hardwood lump with 2 or 3 olive wood or oak chunks. Keep the smoke very light — this is delicate fish.

Kettle A — fish and octopus. Direct, high, and the grate scrupulously clean and oiled. A full chimney runs this high-heat session for one to two hours; keep a reserve lit.

Kettle B — souvlaki, vegetables, flatbread, halloumi. Two-zone, medium-high. Flatbreads go straight on the grate for a minute a side; vegetables on the indirect side; fruit at the end.

Charcoal: about 1 full chimney per kettle for the main service, plus a reserve chimney.

Grilled Octopus htapodi sti skara
Appetizer · Serves 8 · Braise the day before, then 6 min

Octopus must be tender before it meets the fire. Grilling raw octopus produces something closer to a bicycle tyre. Buy it previously frozen — freezing breaks down the muscle and does half the tenderising for you.

  • 2 octopus, about 5 lb total, cleaned
  • 1 onion, halved · 2 bay leaves · 1 tbsp peppercorns
  • 1 cup red wine vinegar · 1 wine cork (traditional, harmless, probably useless)
  • To finish
  • ⅓ cup olive oil · 3 tbsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tsp dried oregano · lemon · flaky salt
  1. Put the octopus in a heavy pot with the onion, bay, peppercorns and vinegar. No water — it releases plenty of its own.
  2. Cover and cook very gently, on the indirect side of a low fire or in a Dutch oven, 45 to 75 minutes, until a skewer slides into the thickest part of a tentacle with no resistance.
  3. Cool in the liquid, then separate the tentacles. Refrigerate overnight — it firms up and grills far better cold.
  4. Rub with oil and grill over a fierce direct fire, 2 to 3 minutes per side, until the edges char and the suckers crisp.
  5. Cut into lengths, dress with oil, vinegar and oregano, and finish with lemon and flaky salt.

Whole Grilled Branzino
Main · Serves 8 · 16 min
  • 4–6 whole branzino or sea bream, scaled and gutted
  • 3 lemons, sliced · 1 bunch parsley · 6 cloves garlic, sliced
  • Olive oil, coarse salt, black pepper
  • Ladolemono, to finish
  • ½ cup olive oil · ¼ cup lemon juice · 1 tsp dried oregano · salt
  1. Score each fish to the bone, twice on each side. Salt inside and out, including the cuts.

  6. Whisk the ladolemono hard so it emulsifies, and pour it over the fish at the table.

Ouzo Service & Visinada
Drinks · Serves 8

Ouzo is an accompaniment to the meze, not a shot. It is poured over ice with water, it turns milky, and it is sipped slowly across an hour. The Greek convention is two sips of water for every sip of ouzo.

  • Ouzo · per glass
  • 2 oz ouzo or tsipouro · 2 oz cold water · ice
  • Visinada · serves 8
  • 1 cup sour cherry syrup (visinada concentrate)
  • 6 cups cold water or soda · ice · lemon
  1. Pour the ouzo over ice, then add the water and watch it cloud. Serve with the meze, never alone.
  2. For visinada, dilute the syrup roughly 1 part to 6 and serve very cold over ice with a squeeze of lemon. Deep red, sweet-tart, and the drink every Greek adult remembers from childhood.
  3. Also pour: Assyrtiko from Santorini with the fish — bone-dry, high-acid and saline, and the definitive pairing for grilled seafood. Moschofilero for the lighter meze, and cold Mythos or Fix lager throughout.

Mexican Taquiza

Early autumn · Casual fiesta · Two kettles · Rotisserie

A taco party built around a vertical spit. Marinated pork stacked on a homemade trompo, turning slowly and getting shaved as its edges crisp; carne asada seared over mesquite on the second grill; and a table of salsas charred on the same fire. People assemble their own and keep coming back.

The menu

  • Elote · Queso fundido with chorizo
  • Tacos al pastor · Carne asada
  • Frijoles charros · Salsa roja and verde (Chapter 22) · Grilled onions and jalapeños · warm tortillas
  • Grilled pineapple with chilli-lime and cajeta

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with mesquite chunks — the classic Mexican grilling wood, and strong enough that two chunks is plenty.

Kettle A — the trompo. Split baskets with a drip pan between them, and a vertical spit rig (a Trompo King, or a sturdy skewer driven through a thick slice of pineapple as a base). Run 275–350°F for 2½ to 3 hours, shaving the crisp outer edges as they brown. No spit? Grill the marinated slices flat over mesquite and chop them — the flavour is all in the adobo.

Kettle B — asada, salsas, sides. Direct and hot for the steak; the same fire blisters the salsa vegetables; cast iron for fundido and charros; tortillas warmed on the bare grate.

Charcoal: about 1.5 chimneys on A for the long cook, 1 chimney on B, plus mesquite.

Elote
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 12 min
  • 8 ears corn, husked
  • ½ cup Mexican crema · ½ cup mayonnaise

  1. Grill the corn directly over hot coals, turning every 2 minutes, 10 to 12 minutes, until charred in patches on every side.

Queso Fundido with Chorizo
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 15 min
  • 1 lb Oaxaca or Chihuahua cheese, shredded or torn
  • ½ lb Mexican chorizo, casing removed
  • 1 poblano and ½ onion, sliced
  • Warm tortillas, to serve
  1. Brown the chorizo in a cast-iron skillet over direct heat, breaking it up. Add the poblano and onion and cook until soft. Spoon most of the fat out.
  2. Pile the cheese over the top and move the pan to the edge of the fire.
  3. 5 to 7 minutes, until just melted and bubbling at the edges. Take it off before it goes oily and stringy.
  4. Serve in the pan, immediately, with tortillas.
Tacos al Pastor
Main · Serves 8 · 3 hr on the spit
  • 5 lb pork shoulder, sliced ¼ inch thick
  • Al pastor adobo (Chapter 24)
  • ½ lb bacon, for layering
  • 1 pineapple: a thick base slice, plus a crown
  • To serve: corn tortillas, diced onion, cilantro, lime, salsas
  1. Marinate the sliced pork 8 to 12 hours and no longer — even canned pineapple juice will eventually turn the meat mealy.
  2. Drive the vertical skewer through a thick slice of pineapple to make a stable base.
  3. Stack the pork slices flat, largest first, pressing down as you go, with a rasher of bacon every four or five layers for fat. Cap with a chunk of pineapple.
  4. Set over split baskets with a drip pan between. Run 275–350°F for 2½ to 3 hours, adding coals as needed.
  5. When the outer layer is browned and crisp — roughly every 45 minutes — shave the outside off with a long knife into a warm pan, and let the next layer take over. The interior should reach 145–155°F.
  6. Chop the shavings, char a little pineapple alongside, and build tacos with onion, cilantro and lime.
No spit?

Lay the marinated slices flat over a hot mesquite fire, 2 to 3 minutes a side, until the edges blacken, then stack and chop them. You lose the theatre and about ten percent of the texture. Nobody will complain.

Carne Asada
Main · Serves 8 · 10 min
  • 3 lb skirt or flank steak
  • Juice of 4 limes and 1 orange
  • 4 cloves garlic, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp Mexican oregano
  • ½ bunch cilantro, chopped · 2 tbsp oil · salt, pepper
  1. Marinate 2 to 6 hours. Citrus marinades work fast; overnight will turn the surface chalky.
  2. Pat dry and salt again just before grilling.
  3. Over the hottest part of the fire: 3 to 4 minutes per side, pulling at 130°F.
  4. Rest 8 minutes, then slice thinly across the grain and chop roughly for tacos.
Frijoles Charros
Side · Serves 8 · 2 hr on the grill
  • 1 lb dried pinto beans, soaked overnight
  • ½ lb bacon, chopped · ½ lb chorizo
  • 1 onion, 4 cloves garlic, 2 jalapeños, diced
  • 3 tomatoes, diced · 6 cups water or stock
  • 1 bunch cilantro, salt
  1. Render the bacon and chorizo in a Dutch oven over direct heat. Add the onion, garlic and jalapeño and cook until soft.
  2. Add the drained beans, tomatoes and water. Bring to a simmer.
  3. Move to the indirect side, lid on, and cook 1½ to 2 hours, until the beans are creamy. Top up with water if they go dry.
  4. Salt only at the end — salted early, the skins toughen and the beans take twice as long.
  5. Stir in chopped cilantro off the heat.

Moroccan Mechoui

Late autumn · Harvest feast · Two kettles · Deflector

A whole-lamb tradition scaled to a shoulder that fits a kettle. Charmoula-marinated lamb cooking slowly over coals for four hours while it's basted with spiced butter; charred vegetable salads; flatbread off the grate; and mint tea poured from a height. Built for cold-weather cooking, when most of the work happens under a closed lid.

The menu

  • Grilled merguez with harissa · Zaalouk
  • Mechoui lamb shoulder · Ras el hanout chicken skewers
  • Khobz (Chapter 20) · Carrot-cumin salad · Charred pepper salad
  • Semolina-almond cake and grilled oranges with orange-blossom yogurt

Grill plan & fuel

Fuel: lump charcoal with oak and a little apple — mild fruit wood keeps the lamb Mediterranean rather than smoky.

Kettle A — the lamb. Deflector in, coals banked or ringed, drip pan of water beneath. Hold 275–300°F for 3½ to 4½ hours. Add a fruitwood chunk early. In cold or windy weather, shelter the kettle and budget 20 to 30 percent more fuel.

Kettle B — everything else. Two-zone, medium-high. Eggplant and peppers charred directly for the salads; merguez and skewers over direct; flatbread on the grate; the cake baked over the deflector at the end.

Charcoal: about 2 chimneys on A, 1.5 on B, plus the cold-weather allowance.

Zaalouk smoky eggplant salad
Appetizer · Serves 8 · 40 min
  • 3 eggplants
  • 4 tomatoes, grated or finely chopped
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tsp cumin, 1 tsp paprika, ½ tsp cayenne
  • ⅓ cup olive oil · juice of 1 lemon
  • ½ bunch cilantro, chopped · salt
  1. Set the whole eggplants directly on the coals or the hot grate and char them until the skins are black all over and the flesh has collapsed — 20 to 30 minutes, turning.

Mechoui Lamb Shoulder
Main · Serves 8 · 4 hr

The traditional version is a whole lamb in a pit. A shoulder, marinated and basted the same way, gives you the same thing at kettle scale: meat you pull apart with your hands and dip in cumin salt.

  • 1 bone-in lamb shoulder, 6–7 lb
  • Charmoula (Chapter 22), half for marinating
  • Spiced basting butter
  • 1 cup (2 sticks) butter, melted
  • 1 tbsp cumin, 1 tbsp paprika, 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 4 cloves garlic, crushed · 1 tsp salt
  • To serve
  • Cumin salt: 3 tbsp toasted ground cumin mixed with 2 tbsp coarse salt
  1. Slash the shoulder deeply in several places and work half the charmoula into the cuts. Marinate overnight.
  2. Set up Kettle A with the deflector and a water pan. Stabilise at 275–300°F.
  3. Lamb on, fat side up. Add a fruitwood chunk in the first hour.
  4. Baste with the spiced butter every 30 to 40 minutes — this is what builds the burnished, crusted exterior that defines mechoui.
  5. Cook 3½ to 4½ hours to 200–205°F, until the blade bone pulls clean.
  6. Rest 30 minutes tented. Serve whole on a board and let people pull it apart, with cumin salt, harissa and the reserved charmoula alongside.

Moroccan Mint Tea atay
Drink · Serves 8

The pour from a height is not showmanship. It aerates the tea and builds the foam on top, which is considered the mark of a properly made glass.

  • 2 tbsp gunpowder green tea
  • 1 very large bunch fresh spearmint
  • ½ cup sugar, or to taste
  • 6 cups water
  1. Put the tea in a pot, add a cup of boiling water, swirl for 30 seconds and pour that first water away — it carries the bitterness.
  2. Add the mint, the sugar and the remaining boiling water. Steep on the edge of the grill for 5 minutes.
  3. Pour a glass, return it to the pot, and repeat twice to mix.
  4. Serve by pouring from a foot or more above the glass, until a head of foam sits on top.
  5. Also pour: a Rhône-style Syrah or Grenache, or a Moroccan red, with the lamb; boukha after.
